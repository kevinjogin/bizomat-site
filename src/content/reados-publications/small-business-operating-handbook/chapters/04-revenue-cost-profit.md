Owners need three views of the same business: what work has been earned, what costs belong to that work and what cash has actually moved. Combining them in one mental number creates false confidence.

## Revenue versus receipts
An invoice may record a sale before a customer pays. A cash receipt may settle an invoice issued in an earlier period. A customer deposit may create a future delivery or refund obligation. Track **invoice issue date**, **due date**, **amount received**, **balance outstanding** and **follow-up status**. The exact timing of revenue recognition depends on the applicable accounting basis and circumstances.

## Cost categories
**Variable costs** generally change with volume: materials, packaging, transaction fees and piecework where relevant. **Fixed or committed costs** may include rent, basic subscriptions and certain overheads. In practice some costs are semi-variable or stepped, such as adding another shift or licence tier. Classify based on the decision being analysed rather than assuming every cost behaves perfectly.

Separate operating expenses from major equipment purchases and financing flows. A new machine may create a large cash outflow without becoming an equally large immediate profit-and-loss expense. Debt repayments include principal, which typically affects cash but not operating profit in the same way as interest. Use proper accounting records for authoritative treatment.

## A simple profit illustration
For a month with **$30,000 of sales**, **$12,000 of materials** and **$13,000 of other recognised business expenses**, simplified profit before tax is:

**$30,000 − $12,000 − $13,000 = $5,000.**

That does not mean $5,000 is safe to withdraw. The business may have overdue invoices, tax payments, loan principal, purchases of assets or future delivery commitments.

## Improve the monthly review
Compare actual to budget and previous periods. Identify the top three costs by value and ask whether each creates necessary value, whether the terms can be improved and whether the activity is profitable once direct costs and required labour are included.

Track gross margin or contribution margin consistently. **Gross profit** and **contribution** are not interchangeable unless the expense classification matches. Clearly define the metric on every dashboard so trends remain comparable.

## Practical owner review
Ask: How many invoices are overdue? What is the ageing of receivables? How much of reported sales have been collected? Which costs rise faster than revenue? Are discounts eroding contribution? Are tax and cash commitments separately funded?

## Exercise
Take the last three complete months. Prepare one table with **sales recorded | cash received | direct costs | overheads | profit before tax | closing cash | unpaid invoices**. Explain every large difference between profit and changes in cash. If you cannot reconcile it, that is an immediate bookkeeping improvement task.
