## About this book

An injection-moulded part is decided twice: once by the designer who draws it and again by the toolmaker who cuts the steel to make it. Most of the expensive problems in moulding come from the gap between the two. A wall that is thicker in one place than another sinks and warps. A rib that is too thick shows through on the cosmetic face. A snap hook strained past its limit cracks on the first assembly. A tolerance copied from a machined part cannot be held in plastic at any price. Each of these is predictable with a little arithmetic and the material supplier's data.

This book is for designers and engineers who model plastic parts in CAD and need them to mould well, assemble reliably and pass first-off inspection. It assumes familiarity with CAD and basic mechanics, but no previous moulding experience. Each chapter takes one design decision, explains the physics behind it, applies it to the running case with real numbers and ends with an exercise and its worked answer.

## The running case

The book follows a fictional **polycarbonate access cover** for a small steel control box on a conveyor. The cover is 160 mm long, 100 mm wide and 20 mm deep, with a nominal wall of 2.5 mm. Two cantilever snap hooks hold one end; two screw bosses take thread-cutting screws at the other. A grid of ribs on the underside keeps the large flat top from flexing or warping. Annual demand is about 8,000 covers. The material is an unreinforced, easy-flow polycarbonate, and the book uses one published grade's typical data sheet values as its reference properties.

All dimensions, quantities, process settings, prices and results are invented for teaching. This is an illustration. They are not KEVOS® projects, quotations or measured data. Money is in Australian dollars, and each example states whether GST is included.

## Source limits and professional responsibility

The supplied CAD textbook informed the topic review. The method, case, calculations and figures are original. Design guideline values are taken from named material-supplier publications and attributed where used; they are general guidance from one supplier for its own materials, and other materials and suppliers may differ. Typical data sheet values are not specifications, and real parts should be designed with the supplier's current data for the chosen grade and colour.

This book is educational. Parts that carry safety-critical loads, contain electrical equipment, contact food or medicine, or must meet flammability or other product standards have requirements beyond its scope. Qualified review, the moulder's and toolmaker's own expertise and testing of real parts govern real work.
