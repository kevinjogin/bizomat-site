## About this book

The National Construction Code lets you comply in two ways: follow the Deemed-to-Satisfy Provisions, or show by another route that the Performance Requirements are met. That second route, the Performance Solution, is what makes the code performance-based. It is also widely misunderstood. Owners hear of it as a way around an inconvenient rule. Some designers treat it as a report that is written after the design is fixed. Neither view survives contact with the process the code now requires.

This book explains that process from the inside, using one small and deliberately ordinary example. It is written for engineers, designers and project managers who commission or review Performance Solutions, and for owners who will live with their conditions. It assumes you can follow simple arithmetic and are willing to read code clauses carefully. Fire engineering theory is introduced only as far as the example needs.

## The running case

This is an illustration. A precision-engineering business is fitting out a single-storey Class 8 workshop, 50 m by 30 m, in regional New South Wales, with a separate Class 5 office behind a fire wall. Inside the workshop is a 190 m² storage mezzanine, 19 m by 10 m, with its deck 3.0 m above the floor. The owner wants the mezzanine's only stair at its east end, where the stair foot lands beside an external exit and away from forklift routes.

With shelving on the mezzanine, the farthest point is about 28 m from the top of that stair, measured along the aisles. The Deemed-to-Satisfy limit is 20 m. Moving the stair to the middle of the mezzanine's south edge complies, at about 18.5 m. The owner asks whether the east-end stair can be kept with a Performance Solution. The chapters follow that question from the first reading of the Performance Requirement to a final report with conditions.

The same building is the running case of the companion Library book on building approval for factory projects, where the Deemed-to-Satisfy answer was chosen. This book shows what the other path involves.

## Source limits and professional responsibility

Code text comes from NCC 2022 Volume One, read on NCC Online in October 2026, because NSW adopts NCC 2025 only from 1 May 2027. Clause numbers and requirements may differ in NCC 2025 and in other states' variations. The ABCB's Fire Safety Verification Method Standard and Australian Fire Engineering Guidelines are named but were not read, so no values are taken from them.

Every evacuation input in this book, including detection times, walking speeds and flow rates, is a teaching assumption chosen to make the arithmetic visible. None is a design value. A real Performance Solution for fire safety must be prepared by an appropriately qualified and, where the law requires, registered fire safety engineer, with inputs drawn from recognised data and agreed in the brief. This book is educational and is not advice on any building.
