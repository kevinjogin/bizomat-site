## About this book

A noisy machine is a problem with a source, a path and a receiver. The source makes sound power; the path carries it, through the air of the room, over or around obstacles, through walls and panels, and along the structure the machine stands on; the receiver is a person at a work position, whose ears accumulate a daily dose. Every noise control measure acts on one of those three, and every one can be estimated with a few numbers before money is spent: how much sound power, how far away, how much absorption, how heavy a panel, how large a gap, how soft a mount.

This book works through machine noise control in the order an engineer meets it: decibels and how they combine, sound power and sound pressure, frequency and weighting, daily exposure, sound in the room, barriers, enclosures, ventilation openings and silencers, vibration isolation, and measuring and verifying the result. Each chapter ends with an exercise and a worked answer.

It suits mechanical and facilities engineers, workshop managers, safety advisers and designers of machines. It assumes school algebra and logarithms. Welding Health and Safety by the Numbers covers noise as one hazard among many in a welding shop; this book is about designing the controls.

## The running case

Every chapter follows one fictional installation. This is an illustration. CP-30 is a 30 kW rotary screw air compressor, supplied without an acoustic canopy, standing on the floor, clear of the walls, at one end of a fabrication workshop 24 m long, 16 m wide and 7 m high, with bare steel walls, a concrete floor and a metal roof. A fitter's workbench is 4 m away, where one worker spends six hours of most days. The compressor's sound power spectrum, the room's absorption and every level in the book are illustrations chosen to show the method.

The installation and its numbers are invented; none describes KEVOS® work or a real workplace. Prices, where they appear, are illustrations in Australian dollars excluding GST.

## Source limits and professional responsibility

This book is educational. The estimates in it are the simplified relationships engineers use for a first design, and real installations differ from them by several decibels. A real noise assessment is made by a competent person with calibrated instruments, under the work health and safety laws and codes of practice of the jurisdiction concerned, and its controls are verified by measurement. Where this book names a regulation or standard, the current adopted version governs, not this book's summary.
