## About this book

A roof light is a hole in a roof that is meant to let light in and keep everything else out. It has to carry the rain from the roof above around itself, hold its glass against wind and its own weight, catch the condensation that forms on its cold underside, keep the room below from overheating in summer, and survive decades of sun and temperature swings without leaking. It also has to be safe for the people who install and later work near it, because a roof light is one of the commonest places people fall through a roof.

Factory-made roof lights come with the maker's tested details. Many roof lights are still designed and built for one opening: a hipped or pitched glazed light over a stair, a kitchen or a hall, made by a sheet metal shop and a glazier from folded flashings, extruded or folded bars and cut glass. This book is for that work. It sizes the roof light, details its kerb and flashings, works out the glass and bars, develops the geometry of hipped and pitched lights with their hips, jacks, ridges and valleys, and then deals with condensation, heat, movement, opening sashes, safety and testing. Every chapter works a problem through the running case with numbers that can be checked, shows how a mistake is detected and corrected, and ends with an exercise and its worked answer.

## Prerequisites and how to use the book

It assumes basic trigonometry, some experience reading building drawings and an understanding of folded sheet metal. Chapters 1 and 2 cover size and the kerb, Chapter 3 glass and bars, Chapters 4 and 5 the geometry of hipped and pitched lights, Chapter 6 condensation, Chapter 7 heat and movement, and Chapter 8 opening sashes, safety and testing. Profiles, girths and flashing mitres are covered in *Architectural Sheet Metal by the Numbers*, and flows of rain in *Roof Drainage by the Numbers*; this book links to them rather than repeating them.

## The running case

The book follows **RL-24**, a fictional hipped glazed roof light over the open-plan kitchen and living room of a single-storey house extension. The room is 7.5 m by 5.6 m, 42 m² of floor. The extension's roof is metal deck at a 5° pitch. The roof light sits on a timber kerb whose inside opening is **2,400 mm** across the slope by **1,200 mm** down it. Its glass is pitched at **25°** on all four faces, so it has two long trapezoidal faces, two triangular hipped ends, four hip bars and a ridge **1,200 mm** long. It is glazed with laminated glass on aluminium bars, with folded aluminium flashings to the kerb.

All dimensions, loads, prices and results are invented for teaching. This is an illustration. None of it describes a KEVOS® client, supplier or project. Money, where it appears, is in Australian dollars including GST.

## Source limits and professional responsibility

The supplied 1922 trade course informed the range of subjects: curbs, bars, condensation gutters, hips and valleys and opening sashes. The methods here are modern restatements with original examples. This book is educational. Glass selection for overhead glazing, structural adequacy, wind actions, energy performance and weatherproofing for a real building must follow the National Construction Code, AS 1288, the wind loading standard and the makers' instructions, and be designed or checked by a competent person and approved where the law requires. Roof work is work at height and must be planned and carried out under the work health and safety law that applies.
