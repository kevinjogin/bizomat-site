## About this book

Manufacturers of configurable products live and die by their quotes. A customer wants a bench 2.1 m long with a sink on the left and two undershelves, and wants a price today. The estimator looks up the last similar quote, adjusts it, checks a few costs, types it into a template and sends it two days later. Sometimes the price is wrong, sometimes the configuration cannot be built, and sometimes the customer has already bought elsewhere. Configure-price-quote, usually shortened to CPQ, is the discipline of turning that process into rules, so that a valid configuration, a correct price and a clear quote come out in minutes.

This book is for owners, sales engineers, estimators and product managers at manufacturers of configurable products. It does not sell any particular software. It shows how to measure quoting, model options and constraints, build prices from cost, write quotes customers trust, connect accepted quotes to orders and drawings, control discounts, keep rules and prices current and measure whether the system works. Each chapter works one decision through the running case with real numbers and ends with an exercise and its worked answer.

## The running case

The book follows a fictional fabricator of **stainless-steel benches for food factories**. The range covers eight lengths from 900 mm to 3,000 mm, two depths, up to two undershelves, three splashback options, optional castors and four sink positions: 1,152 combinations, of which 600 can be built. The firm quotes about 120 benches a month, wins about 40% of them, and each quote has taken an estimator about 45 minutes.

All quantities, costs, prices and results are invented for teaching. This is an illustration. None of it describes a KEVOS® project or product. Money is in Australian dollars and each example states whether GST is included.

## How to use the book

Read Chapters 1 to 3 in order: they build the measures, the option model and the price model that the rest depend on. Chapters 4 to 8 can then be read as needed. The companion book [CAD Automation by the Numbers](/reados/publications/cad-automation-by-the-numbers/) covers generating models and drawings from rules, which Chapter 5 connects to.

## Source limits and professional responsibility

A book on developing CAD software products was supplied with the request for this series. It informed only the emphasis on planning a tool around its users' workflow. The structure, case, calculations and figures here are original. Pricing, tax and consumer law points are general information, not financial, tax or legal advice. A configurator produces exactly the products and prices its rules allow, so the rules need the same engineering and commercial review as any price list or design standard.
