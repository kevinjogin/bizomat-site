A book becomes useful when it changes the next operating week. Do not begin by making a complex dashboard. Begin with a small number of accurate measures and a scheduled decision meeting.

## The seven operating measures
1. **Cash available:** reconciled and net of reserved obligations.
2. **Minimum thirteen-week projected cash:** the lowest forecast position and its date.
3. **Outstanding receivables:** including overdue amounts and ageing.
4. **Contribution performance:** by product or service, consistently calculated.
5. **Committed obligations:** bills, payroll and taxes coming due.
6. **Demand evidence:** qualified enquiries, orders, repeat purchases and validation results.
7. **Owner capacity:** workload, recovery and critical single-person dependencies.

Each measure needs a source, an owner, update frequency and action threshold. A metric with no next action is decoration.

## Thirty-day sequence
**Week one — establish reality.** Reconcile records, list customers and obligations, write the one-page business model and complete the owner workload map. Do not buy new systems yet.

**Week two — make cash visible.** Calculate core contribution and approximate break-even, build the thirteen-week forecast, document the next payment dates and identify any funding or compliance gap. Validate assumptions with actual records.

**Week three — test and simplify.** Choose one uncertain customer question for a low-cost seven-day experiment. Select one repeated administrative task for an AI-assisted pilot, using approved data only. Document a handoff procedure for a task previously dependent on the owner.

**Week four — compare and decide.** Review experiment evidence, the forecast-versus-actual cash variance, time saved after checking and owner capacity. Decide what to continue, change or stop. Assign no more than three major improvements for the following month.

## Weekly 30-minute meeting
Minutes 0–5: owner capacity and urgent commitments. Minutes 5–15: receipts, outflows, forecast minimum and overdue invoices. Minutes 15–22: customer evidence and delivery constraints. Minutes 22–27: improvement tests and AI quality. Minutes 27–30: decisions, owners and due dates. Record actions as **what | why | owner | due | evidence of completion**.

## Decision rights and escalation
Write who can approve routine purchases, customer discounts, public communications, payments, system access and non-standard commitments. Set limits proportionate to business size and risk. Schedule escalation when forecast cash falls below the reserve floor or when compliance deadlines may be missed.

## One-page review template
**What changed this week?** Summarise facts. **Which numbers moved?** Show actual and plan. **What may fail next?** Name the assumption. **What did we learn?** Record customer evidence or trial results. **What will we do?** Assign the next action. **How will we know?** Define measurable evidence.

## A considered improvement principle
Change the smallest process that removes a real constraint. Adding a customer channel, technology product or staff member without knowing what is constraining performance can add cost and cognitive load. Prefer documented, reversible trials until the evidence supports a larger commitment.

## Workbook completion checklist
Maintain an owner capacity map, one-page offer brief, funding estimate, contribution and break-even worksheet, thirteen-week forecast, compliance calendar, AI policy and a 30-day decisions log. These are living records; review them, do not file them away.
