## About this book

Leading a team in the Australian Public Service is a different job from doing the team's work well. A new team leader inherits a workload, a set of people, a service standard and a body of rules about recruitment, performance, conduct, safety and leave. Most people decisions are made under those rules, by someone with the power to make them, and recorded in a way that can be reviewed later. A manager who knows where the rules are and where their own role ends can make better decisions sooner and with more confidence.

This book follows one new manager through the first year of leading a client services team. It starts with the rules behind every people decision, then moves through planning the work, recruiting on merit, onboarding and probation, setting expectations, managing underperformance, suspected misconduct, psychosocial safety and workload, flexible work, leave and attendance, and finally developing people and planning succession. Each chapter ends with an exercise and a worked answer.

The chapters use **the Client Services Section of the Northfield Regional Infrastructure Office**, a fictional agency, as a continuous example. Its manager, staff, workload, service standard, incidents and procedures are invented for teaching. They are not a real agency, team or person, a BIZOMAT project or a claim about how any agency works. Pay, leave, working arrangements and probation periods are set by each agency's enterprise agreement and procedures, so the book gives no figures for them.

## An independent treatment

This is general education, not legal, employment or work health and safety advice, and not an official publication of any agency. Law and policy change, and every agency has its own enterprise agreement, delegations and procedures. Before you act, check the version of each rule in force and your agency's own documents, and ask your People, HR, legal or WHS area when the consequences matter.
