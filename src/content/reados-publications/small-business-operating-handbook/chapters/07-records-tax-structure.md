A sustainable business must know what it is legally, what records it must keep and which payments are obligations rather than discretionary costs. Administrative gaps often appear only when cash is tight, making them harder to resolve.

## Structure affects risk and administration
Common Australian operating structures include sole trader, partnership, company and trusts used in different arrangements. These differ in legal identity, personal liability, tax, control, obligations and costs. A company is not simply a larger sole trader, and a trust is not the same legal arrangement as a company. Seek professional advice before formation, restructuring or transferring assets or intellectual property.

Review the structure at material changes: taking investors, hiring, acquiring another business, transferring licences, taking debt or preparing a sale. Changing structure may have costs and consequences.

## Keep an evidence trail
Maintain dated invoices, receipts, bank statements, contracts, payroll records where relevant, tax correspondence and asset registers. Store private files with limited access, sensible naming and backups. Reconciling monthly is easier than reconstructing decisions at tax time.

## Taxes and GST
If registered for GST, some cash received may include amounts to be accounted for through Business Activity Statements, subject to input tax credits and the entity's circumstances. This cash is not automatically free to spend. A separate tax provision or account can help prevent unplanned shortfalls, but amounts must be based on actual obligations.

Confirm GST registration thresholds, accounting basis, record retention periods, deductible treatment and due dates with current Australian Taxation Office guidance. Never substitute a generic percentage of turnover for an accurate calculation.

## Employing people
Consider wages, PAYG withholding, superannuation, leave, workers compensation, payroll systems and payroll tax where applicable. Employee classification and award obligations require proper review. Rules and commencement dates may change; consult current ATO, Fair Work and state revenue guidance before implementing payroll changes. Do not rely on an old presentation for compliance deadlines.

## Compliance calendar
Use rows **Obligation | Authority | Reporting period | Due date | Amount expected | Responsible person | Evidence lodged | Paid date**. At least monthly, reconcile it against payroll, accounts and the cash forecast.

## Strong approval routine
A payment should have an authorised purpose, evidence, budget or business justification, correct bank details independently verified where changed, and a clear record of the approver. Small teams can separate preparation from approval even when perfect segregation is impractical.

## Exercise
Prepare a one-page compliance inventory: business registration, insurance, licences, taxation, records, contracts and employment. For each mark **applies**, **not applicable with reason** or **needs confirmation**. Assign a date for every unresolved item.

*This chapter is general education. Obtain current official guidance and professional legal, tax or accounting advice.*
