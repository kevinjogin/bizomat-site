A pinion with few teeth is attractive: it gives a high ratio in a small space. Below a certain tooth number, however, the generating tool cuts away part of the tooth's root as it forms the involute. The tooth is weakened exactly where bending stress is highest, and part of its working profile disappears. That loss is called undercut.

Profile shift is the standard remedy. This chapter explains why undercut happens, how much shift prevents it, and what shifting does to tooth thickness and centre distance.

## Why small pinions undercut

Gears are commonly generated by a rack-shaped or hob tool whose straight-sided teeth reproduce the basic rack. As the blank rotates and the tool moves, the tool's teeth sweep out involute flanks. The involute exists only outside the base circle. On a pinion with few teeth, the base circle is close to the pitch circle, and the tool's tip line reaches inside the point where its flank meets the base circle. The tool then cuts into the root, removing material the involute needed.

For standard full-depth teeth with no shift, the theoretical minimum number of teeth that avoids undercut is:

**z_min = 2 / sin² α**

At α = 20°, sin² α ≈ 0.11698, so **z_min ≈ 17.1**. A standard 20° pinion needs about 17 or more teeth to escape undercut entirely. The conveyor pinion, with 20 teeth, is clear.

## Profile shift moves the tool outward

**Profile shift**, also called addendum modification, moves the generating tool away from the gear centre by x × m, where x is the profile shift coefficient. The same tool still cuts an involute from the same base circle, but uses a part of the curve farther from the base circle. The tooth becomes thicker at the root and pitch circle and pointier at the tip.

The minimum shift that avoids undercut on a pinion with z teeth is, for standard full-depth teeth:

**x_min = 1 − (z × sin² α) / 2**

For a 14-tooth pinion:

**x_min = 1 − (14 × 0.11698) / 2 ≈ 1 − 0.8189 ≈ 0.181**

A designer would typically round up, for example to x = 0.2, and then check that the tooth tip has not become too sharp.

## What the shift does to the pinion

For a 14-tooth pinion of module 3 mm with x = 0.2:

- the pitch diameter is unchanged at **3 × 14 = 42 mm**, because pitch diameter depends only on module and tooth number;
- the tip diameter grows to **3 × (14 + 2 + 2 × 0.2) = 49.2 mm**, compared with 48 mm unshifted;
- the root diameter grows to **3 × (14 − 2.5 + 2 × 0.2) = 35.7 mm**, compared with 34.5 mm;
- the tooth thickness at the pitch circle grows from π × 3 / 2 ≈ 4.712 mm to:

**s = m × (π/2 + 2 × x × tan α) = 3 × (1.5708 + 2 × 0.2 × 0.36397) ≈ 5.149 mm**

The thicker root improves bending strength, which is often a second reason to shift a pinion even when it would not undercut.

## Shifts change the centre distance

If one gear is shifted positively and its mate negatively by the same amount, the sum is zero and the standard centre distance still applies. This is a common way to strengthen a small pinion at the expense of a large gear that has strength to spare.

If the shifts do not sum to zero, the gears no longer mesh correctly at the standard centre distance. The working pressure angle α′ is found from the involute function:

**inv α′ = 2 × tan α × (x₁ + x₂) / (z₁ + z₂) + inv α**

and the working centre distance from:

**a′ = a × cos α / cos α′**

Take the 14-tooth pinion with x₁ = 0.2 meshing with an unshifted 43-tooth gear, module 3 mm. The standard centre distance is 3 × 57 / 2 = 85.5 mm. Then:

**inv α′ = 2 × 0.36397 × 0.2 / 57 + 0.014904 ≈ 0.017459**

Solving for the angle whose involute function equals 0.017459 gives **α′ ≈ 21.04°**, found by iteration or a solver. The working centre distance is:

**a′ = 85.5 × 0.939693 / cos 21.04° ≈ 86.085 mm**

The housing must be bored 0.585 mm wider than the standard figure. Getting this wrong is a classic cause of a pair that jams or rattles when a shifted replacement gear is fitted into a housing built for standard gears.

## Profile shift is a design decision

Profile shift can avoid undercut, balance bending strength or sliding between pinion and gear, adjust a centre distance to fit an existing housing, or tune contact ratio. It must be recorded on the drawing, because two gears with the same module and tooth number but different shifts are not interchangeable. Chapter 10 returns to what the drawing must state.

## Exercise: a 12-tooth pinion

Calculate the minimum profile shift for a 12-tooth, 20° pinion, then the tooth thickness at the pitch circle for module 2 mm if the shift is rounded up to x = 0.3.

The minimum shift is **1 − (12 × 0.11698) / 2 ≈ 0.298**. With x = 0.3 and m = 2, the tooth thickness is **2 × (1.5708 + 2 × 0.3 × 0.36397) ≈ 2 × 1.7892 ≈ 3.578 mm**, compared with π × 2 / 2 ≈ 3.142 mm unshifted. Check whether the tip remains acceptably wide before finalising the shift.
