## About this book

A hydraulic system turns a small electric motor into a large, controllable force. It does that with a few simple relations: force is pressure times area, speed is flow divided by area, and power is pressure times flow. Most hydraulic trouble in a small workshop comes from forgetting one of them at the wrong moment. A cylinder is sized for the rated force but the frame is never checked at the relief setting. A return filter is sized for the pump's flow, not the larger flow that leaves the cap end. A speed control is added to slow a stroke and quietly doubles the pressure on the rod side.

This book follows one fictional machine through design, build and service. A small pump-repair workshop builds a **four-post hydraulic press rated at 200 kN** to press bushes, bearings and wear rings into and out of pump housings. It has a 125 mm bore cylinder with a 70 mm rod and a 300 mm stroke, a fast approach, a slow pressing stroke and a quick return. Each chapter adds one part of the design and checks it with numbers, and each ends with an exercise and a worked answer.

## Who it is for and how to use it

The book is for maintenance technicians, machine builders, mechanical engineers and owners who specify, build, modify or look after hydraulic equipment. You need workshop arithmetic, the idea of stress as force over area, and a calculator. No fluid mechanics course is assumed; each relation is introduced where it is first used.

Read the chapters in order the first time, because the press grows as you go. Afterwards each chapter stands as a reference for its own step. Where a chapter uses a BIZOMAT tool, it says what the tool calculates, which inputs matter and where its model stops.

## The running case

This is an illustration. The workshop, its press, its jobs, its measurements and every money figure are invented for teaching. They are not BIZOMAT projects, clients or results. Money is in Australian dollars excluding GST.

The press has to deliver 200 kN at the end of a 300 mm stroke, approach the work at about 54 mm/s, press at about 11 mm/s and return at about 79 mm/s. Its power unit has two fixed pumps, a 4 kW motor and a 120 L reservoir. Chapters 1 to 6 design it, Chapter 7 deals with its stored energy and the weight of its platen, Chapter 8 with its oil, and Chapter 9 commissions it and diagnoses its first faults.

## Sources and professional responsibility

The relations used are standard engineering mechanics and fluid flow, cited to university course notes and public technical guidance. Standards are named by number and scope only; no clause, table or value is quoted from a standard. Component ratings, such as a pump's viscosity limits or a filter's pressure drop, are written as illustrations of the kind of figure a maker publishes; real work uses the actual maker's data.

Hydraulic presses store large amounts of energy and can crush, eject parts and inject oil through skin. This book is educational. It does not approve any design, setting or procedure for a real machine. Real presses need a risk assessment, guarding and control systems designed by competent people, the component makers' instructions, and the work health and safety duties that apply in your state or territory.
