## About this book

A sheet metal shop lives on its quotes. Price too high and the work goes elsewhere; price too low and the shop wins jobs that lose money, often without knowing it until the year's accounts arrive. Most of what decides a quote is countable from the drawing: the blanks and the sheets they nest on, the metres of cut and the number of pierces, the bends and the tool changes, the joints, the finished area, the setups. The rest is rates and judgement: what an hour of each machine and person really costs, how much margin the work can bear, and what can go wrong.

This book works through a sheet metal quote in the order an estimator builds it. It covers scope and what a quote must say, material and nesting, cutting, folding and forming, joining and assembly, finishing, setups and batch size, rates, overheads, margin and GST, risk and checking, and comparing quotes with the job's actual costs so that the next quote is better.

It suits estimators, owners, production managers and engineers in small and medium fabrication shops. It assumes familiarity with sheet metal processes; *Sheet Metal Parts by the Numbers* covers the folded parts and *Sheet Metal Joints by the Numbers* the joints that this book prices.

## The running case

Every chapter follows one fictional quote. This is an illustration. A fabricator is asked to quote for EN-700, the outdoor equipment enclosure of *Sheet Metal Joints by the Numbers*: a 700 × 500 × 250 mm body in 1.2 mm galvanised steel, a 2.0 mm aluminium door, a 2.0 mm steel equipment plate on two brackets, a rain hood and a cable plate. The customer asks for prices at 20, 50 and 200 off.

The fabricator, the customer and every price, rate and time in this book are invented illustrations in Australian dollars, excluding GST unless stated; none describes KEVOS® work, a real business or real market prices. Real quotes must use a shop's own costs, rates and records.

## Source limits and professional responsibility

The arithmetic of quoting is simple and every calculation in this book was checked by running it. The numbers that matter, a shop's rates, its machines' speeds, its overheads, its margins, are its own and change with time; the method, not the figures, is what transfers. Pricing decisions also carry legal obligations, including consumer and competition law administered by the ACCC and GST law administered by the ATO, and a business should take its own accounting and legal advice. This book is educational and is not financial, tax or legal advice.
