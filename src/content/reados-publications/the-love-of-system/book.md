## About this book

Engineers like well-made things. A robot that loads a lathe in eleven seconds, a dashboard that shows every machine's state in real time, a measuring machine that reads to a tenth of a micron, a procedure that covers every job the shop has ever made: each has a beauty that people who build things recognise at once. That appreciation is part of what makes good engineers. It is also behind some of the worst purchases small businesses make.

Adam Smith noticed this in 1759, in *The Theory of Moral Sentiments*. He observed that people often value the fitness of a contrivance, the neat way its parts serve their purpose, more than the purpose itself. His example was a man who sells a watch that loses two minutes a day to buy one that keeps perfect time, though he is no more punctual afterwards; what he wanted was the perfection of the machine. Smith did not think this was simply foolish. The same love of order and contrivance drives much of human industry and invention. But he also saw its danger, especially in what he called the man of system: a person so taken with the beauty of his own plan that he tries to arrange people as easily as a hand arranges pieces on a chessboard, forgetting that each piece has its own principle of motion.

This book applies Smith's observation to the decisions a small engineering business makes about machines, software, measurement and procedures. It shows how to name the end a proposal must serve, price that end, and test each option against realistic use. It also shows how to introduce systems so they work with people, and how to decide which work to fix in precise rules and which to leave to trained judgement.

## Who it is for and how to use it

The book is for owners, engineers and managers who propose, approve or implement improvements in small manufacturing and engineering businesses. It assumes familiarity with workshop operations and simple business cases.

Read the chapters in order. Chapter 1 sets out Smith's observation and the case. Chapter 2 shows how to name and price the end. Chapters 3 to 5 test three kinds of contrivance: an automation cell, a precision upgrade and a software system. Chapter 6 covers the man of system and how to introduce change. Chapter 7 covers rules and judgement, and Chapter 8 how to keep the love of system working for the business. Each chapter ends with an exercise and a worked answer.

The calculations use several BIZOMAT tools: machine hour rate, improvement value, automation business case, equipment investment appraisal, measurement uncertainty budget, process or software readiness, build or buy software, change impact assessment, before and after comparison, and business case with NPV. Each chapter states what the tool calculates and the limits of its model.

## The running case

This is an illustration. A precision machining job shop with twelve people makes turned and milled parts in batches of a few to a few hundred for mining equipment, food machinery and pump makers. The owner is an engineer who loves good machinery and good systems. In one year he receives, or produces, three proposals: a robot cell to load the main CNC lathe, a scheduling and machine-monitoring system with live dashboards, and a complete set of standard work instructions to be introduced across the shop in one go. A fourth question, whether to upgrade the coordinate measuring machine, arrives from a sales representative.

Each proposal is attractive as a contrivance. The book follows how each is judged against the end it is meant to serve, and what the shop actually does.

All businesses, people, prices, times and results in the case are invented for teaching. They are not BIZOMAT work. Money values are in Australian dollars and exclude GST unless stated.

## Source limits and professional responsibility

This book is educational. Investment decisions should use the business's own approved discount rate, tax advice and financial information. Automation and machine changes must meet work health and safety obligations, including guarding and risk assessment, and need competent design and commissioning.

Smith's works are in the public domain and are paraphrased. The supplied Companion informed the reading of Smith; the structure, case, calculations and exercises are original.
