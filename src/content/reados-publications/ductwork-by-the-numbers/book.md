## About this book

Ductwork moves air: fresh air into a building, fume and dust out of a workshop, hot air away from a process. Every duct is sized, shaped and made with numbers. Its cross-section sets the air's velocity for a given flow; its length, roughness and fittings set the pressure the fan must supply; its transitions and branches decide whether air goes where it is meant to; and its sheet and supports decide what it costs and whether it stays up. Most of the numbers are geometry, and the rest are a little fluid mechanics that a spreadsheet handles easily.

This book works through those numbers in the order a ducted system is designed and made. It covers flow, area and velocity; round and rectangular equivalents; branches and the main; friction in straight duct; fittings and velocity pressure; adding the losses to a fan duty; transitions treated for their effect on the air; fans, system curves and the fan laws; sheet, mass, supports and cost; and measuring and balancing the finished system. Flat-pattern development of duct fittings is a sheet metal topic in its own right and is not covered here.

It suits mechanical designers, drafters, fabricators and maintenance engineers. It assumes school algebra and a spreadsheet. READOS's *Tanks and Hoppers by the Numbers* applies the same mensuration to vessels and is a natural companion.

## The running case

Every chapter follows one fictional system. This is an illustration. A fabrication workshop installs a dust and fume extraction system, EX-1, serving three booths, B1, B2 and B3, each drawing 0.30 m³/s through its hood. B1, the furthest from the fan, feeds the start of the main; B2 and B3 join it at junctions N2 and N3. The main runs to a collector and fan, and the fan discharges through a short stack. The ventilation designer has specified a minimum duct velocity of 18 m/s to keep the dust moving.

The workshop, the system and its numbers are invented; none describes KEVOS® work or a real installation. Prices are illustrations in Australian dollars, excluding GST. Pressure-loss coefficients for fittings are labelled as illustrations: real values come from the fitting's manufacturer or a recognised design reference.

## Source limits and professional responsibility

The geometry in this book is exact and every calculation was checked by running it. The fluid mechanics uses standard equations with stated simplifications. The design of a real ventilation or extraction system, including its hood design, capture and transport velocities, fan selection, fire and smoke requirements, and the protection of workers from airborne contaminants, is governed by the National Construction Code, Australian Standards such as AS 1668.2 for mechanical ventilation and AS 4254 for ductwork, and work health and safety law, including the workplace exposure standards published by Safe Work Australia. This book is educational. Real systems must be designed and commissioned by competent people to the requirements that apply.
