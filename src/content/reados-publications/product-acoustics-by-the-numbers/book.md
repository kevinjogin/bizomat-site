## About this book

Many products have an acoustic job, whether their designers think of it that way or not. An appliance must be quiet enough to live with; a tool must not be so loud that its user needs ear muffs; a connected device must speak clearly and hear its user over its own noise. Each of those jobs depends on parts that are small compared with the wavelengths of sound: cavities, ports, grilles, membranes, drivers and the gaps between panels. At that scale, acoustics becomes a matter of circuits, of masses, springs and resistances that can be calculated on a page before a prototype is made.

This book works through product acoustics in the order a designer meets it: writing the acoustic requirements, acoustic circuits, resonators and ports, small sealed enclosures for drivers, the driver itself, air paths through a product, microphone ports, radiation and directivity, fans and structure-borne noise, and measuring and declaring the result. Each chapter ends with an exercise and a worked answer.

It suits product designers, mechanical and electronics engineers and product managers. It assumes school algebra and some familiarity with simple electrical circuits, which the acoustic analogies borrow. Machine Noise Control by the Numbers deals with noise from installed machines in workshops; this book is about designing the sound of the product itself.

## The running case

Every chapter follows one fictional product. This is an illustration. AP-40 is a floor-standing room air purifier, a cabinet about 250 × 380 mm in plan and 600 mm high, with a backward-curved fan that runs from 500 rpm on its night setting to 1,200 rpm at maximum, a 40 mm loudspeaker for voice prompts, and a microphone near its top for voice commands. Its sound powers, driver parameters and every level in the book are illustrations chosen to show the method.

The product and its numbers are invented; none describes KEVOS® work or a real product. Prices, where they appear, are illustrations in Australian dollars excluding GST.

## Source limits and professional responsibility

This book is educational. The relationships in it are the simplified models designers use for first estimates, and real products differ from them by several decibels, particularly at high frequencies where parts stop being small compared with the wavelength. Real designs are refined with measurement and, where needed, numerical simulation, and declared noise values are measured to the applicable standards. Products sold in Australia must meet the safety, electrical and labelling requirements that apply to them; this book does not describe those obligations.
