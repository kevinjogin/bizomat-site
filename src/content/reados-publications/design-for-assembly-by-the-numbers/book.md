## About this book

Most of the cost of assembling a product is decided before anyone picks up a part. It is decided by how many parts the designer drew, how easily each can be grasped and turned the right way, whether it drops into place or must be held, aligned and screwed, and whether it can be put in wrongly. Design for assembly is the discipline of making those decisions on purpose, with numbers, while the design can still change cheaply.

This book follows one fictional product through a full design-for-assembly review. A small Australian manufacturer makes a **spring-loaded indexing plunger**, the IP-16, which machine builders screw into fixtures and rotary tables to lock them in position. The current design, revision A, has **11 parts** and takes about **70 s** to assemble. The company builds about **6,000** a year. The book times it, counts it, questions every part, redesigns it to **6 parts** assembled in about **26 s**, checks that the new design still works and still fits together, works out whether the change pays, and releases it through a pilot build.

## Who it is for and how to use it

The book is for design engineers, manufacturing engineers, production leads and owners of small manufacturing businesses. You need to read a simple mechanical drawing, use a stopwatch and do arithmetic. Each chapter takes one step of the review and ends with an exercise and a worked answer.

Read it in order the first time, because the product changes as you go. Afterwards each chapter stands as a reference for its step. Where a chapter uses a BIZOMAT tool, it says what the tool calculates and where its model stops.

## The running case

This is an illustration. The company, the IP-16, its customers, its times, costs, volumes and defect rates are invented for teaching. They are not BIZOMAT projects, clients or results. Money is in Australian dollars excluding GST, at an illustrative fully loaded labour rate of A$62 an hour.

## The method and its sources

The design for assembly method in this book follows the approach published by Boothroyd, Dewhurst and Knight: find the theoretical minimum number of parts with three questions, estimate the actual assembly time, and compare the two with a design efficiency index. Their published handling and insertion time database is not used here. Every time in this book is an illustrative stopwatch figure of the kind a small company can measure itself, and the chapters show how to measure them properly.

## Professional responsibility

This book is educational. A real product's redesign needs its own drawings, its own load cases and tests, its own risk assessment for the people who assemble it, and approval by people competent to give it. The retention, tolerance and cost checks shown here are the start of that work, not a substitute for it.
