## About this book

Every vehicle that moves material around a factory or yard has to do five things: carry its load, climb, pull, stop and turn, without tipping over or losing grip. Each of those is a short calculation with a handful of inputs: masses, the position of the centre of gravity, a few coefficients, the gradient and the speed. Get the inputs roughly right and the calculations show quickly whether a vehicle, a trailer train or a site rule is reasonable. Leave them out and the first sign of trouble is a tow tractor spinning its wheels halfway up a wet ramp.

This book works through those calculations in the order a vehicle meets them: axle loads, driving resistances, traction, power and energy, gearing, acceleration, stopping, brakes, turning and stability. It is written for engineers, maintenance planners and operations leads who specify, modify or write rules for powered mobile plant. It assumes school physics: forces, moments, friction and the equations of uniform motion. Each chapter ends with an exercise and a worked answer.

## The running case

This is an illustration. A manufacturer moves parts between its warehouse and two assembly lines with a battery-electric tow tractor pulling three parts trailers. The tractor has a mass of 1,800 kg plus a 90 kg driver, a wheelbase of 1,400 mm and its centre of gravity 800 mm behind the front axle and 500 mm above the floor. It drives through the rear axle, on wheels with a dynamic radius of 250 mm. Each trailer weighs 300 kg empty and carries up to 1,000 kg. The route includes a 6% concrete ramp between buildings.

The coefficients used are teaching assumptions, stated where they are used: a rolling resistance coefficient of 0.015 on concrete, adhesion coefficients of 0.6 on clean dry concrete and 0.3 on wet or dusty concrete, and a drivetrain efficiency of 0.85. Real values come from the vehicle and tyre makers and from measurements on the site's own floors.

## Source limits and professional responsibility

The supplied source is an English edition of a German automotive tables book from around 1980, without a title page. It was used only to confirm the scope of standard vehicle mechanics. Its data tables, figures and worked examples are not reproduced, and its engine and electrical material is outside this book.

Tow tractors, forklifts and other powered mobile plant are regulated under work health and safety law, and Safe Work Australia's code of practice on managing the risks of plant sets out the duties involved. This book is educational. Its calculations help frame questions about a vehicle and a route; they do not establish that a vehicle, trailer or brake system is safe. Vehicle selection, modifications and braking performance must follow the manufacturer's specifications and ratings, a site risk assessment and, where required, review by a competent engineer.
