## About this book

The centrifugal pump is the most common machine in industry after the electric motor that drives it. It moves water to cooling towers, washwater to header tanks, chemicals between vessels and condensate back to boilers, and in most plants it uses more electricity than anything else that is not making product. Most pumps were selected once and are rarely questioned afterwards, and many run far from where they should: throttled, oversized, cavitating, or running against closed valves.

This book works through centrifugal pumps with one running case. It sets out what a pump does to the water, how head, flow, power and efficiency relate, how to read a pump's curves, and where a pump will run on a system. It then deals with net positive suction head and cavitation, the affinity laws and speed control, throttling, bypass and impeller trimming, pumps in series and parallel, starting and protecting pumps, and finally selecting a pump on its life-cycle cost. Each chapter calculates with the running case, includes a plausible mistake and how it was found, and ends with an exercise and its worked answer.

It is for mechanical and maintenance engineers, technicians and operators. It assumes school algebra and a calculator or spreadsheet, and the ideas of head, friction and the system curve; the Library’s Pipe Flow by the Numbers sets those out from the beginning, and Water Hammer by the Numbers deals with the surges that pump starts and trips cause.

## The running case

Every chapter follows one fictional pump. This is an illustration. A food processing plant collects washwater in a concrete sump and pumps it, as TP-3 on its drawings, to a header tank on the roof, from which it feeds the plant's wash-down hoses by gravity. The pump is an end-suction centrifugal pump on a plinth beside the sump, lifting from a water level 0.5 to 2.0 m below its centreline, through a foot valve and 6 m of suction pipe, and delivering through 120 m of steel pipe of 105.3 mm bore to a free discharge 18.0 m above its centreline. The plant needs at least 12 L/s. The water is at about 20 °C.

The plant, its pump, the pump's curves, prices and mistakes are invented. None describes BIZOMAT work, a real business or any manufacturer's product.

## How to use the book

Read Chapters 1 to 4 first: they set out the pump's energy conversion, its power and efficiency, its curves and its operating point, which every later chapter uses. Chapters 5 to 9 each deal with one way pumps are protected, controlled or combined, and Chapter 10 brings them together in a selection and a commissioning record. Every calculation was checked by running it.

## Source limits and professional responsibility

The pump curves in this book are illustrations of typical shapes, not any product's data; real selection uses the manufacturer's tested curves, and real performance is confirmed by test. Pumps store and release large amounts of energy, run at high speeds, and handle liquids that may be hot, pressurised or hazardous; their installation, guarding, isolation and electrical work are governed by regulation and must be done by competent people. This book teaches the hydraulics and energy of pumps. It is not a design for any installation, and qualified review governs real work.
