The first weeks in a job shape the next few years. A new employee learns what the work is, what good work looks like and whether it is safe to ask for help. The team learns whether the manager meant what the advertisement said. Probation sits on top of all that: a period in which the agency checks whether the person it engaged is suitable for the duties, and the person finds out whether the job suits them.

This is an illustration. Ruby Nguyen started at NRIO on Monday 5 October 2026 as an ongoing APS 4 Client Service Officer in Marcus Tan's phones and email team. She is new to the APS and spent the previous four years in the customer service centre of a regional council. She is Hannah Lee's first new starter, and her engagement is on probation. This chapter follows her first weeks and the review plan that runs through her probation.

## Engagement sets the terms

Section 22(1) of the [Public Service Act 1999](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00538/latest/text) gives the agency head the power to engage employees, and Chapter 1 traced how that power reaches a delegate. Section 22(2) allows three kinds of engagement: ongoing, for a specified term or task, or for irregular or intermittent duties. Ruby's is the first kind.

Section 22(6) lets an engagement be made "subject to conditions notified to the employee". The conditions it lists include probation, citizenship, formal qualifications, security and character clearances, and health clearances. The word "notified" matters. A condition applies because it was in the offer the employee accepted, so the manager needs to know what that offer said.

This is an illustration. In the week before Ruby starts, Hannah reads her letter of offer. It sets out her classification, the enterprise agreement that covers her and two conditions: probation, and a satisfactory pre-employment character check, which People Branch has already completed. The probation clause gives the length of the period and its end date, as NRIO's conditions and the agreement set them. This book gives no figure, because the length is set by each agency's agreement or conditions.

Pay, leave, hours and working from home also come from the enterprise agreement, not from the Act. When Ruby asks about any of them, Hannah's answer starts with the agreement and People Branch, not with what the team usually does.

## What the Directions require of probation

Section 44 of the [Commissioner's Directions 2022](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2022L00088/latest/text) is short, and its verbs matter:

- **Must.** An agency head engaging an APS employee, other than one engaged for irregular or intermittent duties, "must ensure that the employee is engaged on probation" (s 44(1)).
- **May.** The agency head may waive that requirement if satisfied that there are "reasonable circumstances justifying the waiver" (s 44(2)).
- **Must.** During probation the agency head must ensure that "processes are in place to assess the suitability" of the employee to perform the duties (s 44(3)).

Three points follow. First, probation is the default for non-ongoing employees engaged for a specified term or task, as well as for ongoing employees. Second, the Directions require a process for assessing suitability, not just an end date. Third, the Directions require probation on engagement, and a promotion is not an engagement.

This is an illustration. NRIO's fictional probation procedure, owned by People Branch, supplies the assessment process: reviews at set points, a written record of each, and the manager's recommendation to the delegate before the end date. Tessa Morgan, the new APS 5 who starts on Monday 15 February 2027 (Chapter 3), is not being engaged. She is an ongoing APS employee moving to NRIO on promotion, so People Branch confirms that no probation applies to her. Her onboarding still does.

The end of probation can lead to a decision either way. For an ongoing employee, "failure to meet a condition imposed under subsection 22(6)" is one of the grounds for termination in section 29(3)(f). That decision belongs to a delegate, with People Branch's advice, and a note to section 29 points out that the Fair Work Act 2009 also has rules for termination. The manager's part is to make sure that whatever is decided rests on a fair process and an accurate record.

## Before day one

Most first-day frustrations, such as no login, no desk or nobody expecting the new starter, can be fixed the week before.

This is an illustration. In the week before 5 October, Hannah and Marcus prepare five things.

- **Access and a place.** System accounts, a headset, and a desk beside Ruby's buddy, Sophie Grant, an experienced APS 4 in the team.
- **A first-week timetable,** with names, times and rooms, so that Ruby always knows where to be and whom to ask.
- **The probation dates.** People Branch gives Hannah the end date from Ruby's conditions, and Hannah books every review in both calendars before Ruby arrives.
- **Integrity training.** Section 19(1) of the Directions requires an agency head who engages an ongoing employee to arrange integrity training "within 6 months of being engaged". For Ruby, engaged on 5 October, that means by 5 April 2027. Hannah books her into a November session rather than leave it to the deadline.
- **An interests conversation.** Ruby's former employer is one of the councils that contact NRIO. Section 13(7) of the Act requires her to take reasonable steps to avoid conflicts of interest, real or apparent, and to disclose material personal interests. Hannah plans to raise it in week one, so that any enquiry Ruby feels conflicted about can go to someone else.

One obligation sits with Ruby herself. Section 16 of the [Public Service Regulations 2023](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2023L00368/latest/text) says "APS employees must inform themselves about the Act, this instrument and the Commissioner's Directions." The induction should point her to them, and explain the APS Values and Code of Conduct in plain terms rather than hand over a reading list.

## The first weeks, planned and written down

A new starter cannot meet expectations nobody has stated. Section 49 of the Directions has the agency head ensure that each employee is given "a clear statement of the performance and behaviour expected of the employee", and section 50 lists, among the ways a supervisor upholds effective performance, ensuring the employee has a performance agreement consistent with the agency's corporate plan and the work level standards, which describe the work expected at each classification. For a new starter, the first statement is the onboarding plan itself.

This is an illustration. On her first morning, Hannah gives Ruby a one-page plan based on NRIO's fictional ramp-up expectations for new phone officers. Weeks 1 and 2 are for induction, systems training and listening to calls with Sophie. In weeks 3 and 4 Ruby takes calls with Sophie or Marcus on hand. From week 5 she takes calls alone. Marcus samples ten of her calls a week from week 3 and scores each against NRIO's five-point quality checklist: confirming the caller's identity before discussing a record; giving correct information or finding it; a clear and respectful manner; an accurate case note in the client record; and escalating when needed.

The plan states three checkpoints:

- by the end of week 4, an average handling time of 9 minutes or less per call, and at least 70 per cent of sampled calls meeting the checklist;
- by the end of week 8, 7.5 minutes or less, and at least 85 per cent;
- after that, the standard for experienced officers: at least 90 per cent, with handling times close to the team's 6-minute average.

Quality comes first. A new officer who reaches 6 minutes in week 3 by cutting calls short is a worry, not a success. Ruby's performance agreement, settled by Friday 30 October in line with the section's cycle (Chapter 5), carries the same expectations.

## Probation reviews that assess suitability

Section 44(3) speaks of assessing "suitability" to perform the duties. A practical reading covers whether the person can do the work at the level of the classification, whether they work in line with the APS Values and the Code of Conduct, and how they work with clients and colleagues. A probation review should look at each, with evidence.

This is an illustration. NRIO's fictional procedure sets three reviews: at the end of the first month, at the midpoint of the period, and a final review early enough for the manager's written recommendation to reach the delegate at least four weeks before the end date. Each review uses evidence gathered along the way: Marcus's call samples, handling times from the phone system, examples of good and poor work, and feedback from Sophie and Marcus.

```figure

title: What each probation review records
caption: This is an illustration. NRIO's fictional procedure asks for a short note after every review, shared with the employee within two working days.
- The date and who attended | And the evidence used, such as call samples, handling times and examples
- Progress against each expectation | Met, on track or not yet, with the reason
- What is going well | Specific, so it continues
- What needs work, and the support agreed | Each action with an owner and a date
- The employee's own comments | In their words, added before the note is filed
- The next review date | Already in both calendars
- ? Ask People Branch | Where the record is kept and who receives a copy
- x Anything new at the final review | Every concern should already be in an earlier note
```

Raise a concern when you first see it. Section 50 of the Directions includes giving "clear, honest and timely feedback", and, where performance is unsatisfactory, identifying the problem "at the earliest opportunity" and "maintaining appropriate records". If something concerns you in week three, say so in week three, with an example, and write it down.

## What fair probation looks like

Fair probation tests the person against expectations they knew about, after the training they needed, with feedback in time to act on it. The table shows the difference.

```figure
type: compare
title: Fair probation and probation in name only
caption: Each row compares how one element of probation is handled. The left column describes a process that gives the new starter a fair chance and leaves a record that supports the decision.
cols: Fair probation | Probation in name only
best: 1
rowhead: Element
- Expectations | Written in week one, the same as for other new starters | Learned by getting things wrong
- Training | Given before performance is judged | Assumed
- Feedback | Weekly, specific, with examples | Saved for the review
- Reviews | Booked on day one and held on the date | Postponed whenever it gets busy
- Record | A short note after each review, shared | Memory
- The employee's voice | Comments added to each note | Absent
- The decision | A recommendation the record supports, before the end date | Made, or missed, after it
```

Fairness also means reading results in context. A rough week is not evidence of unsuitability.

This is an illustration. The aggressive call to Sophie on Monday 16 November (Chapter 8) fell in Ruby's seventh week. Hannah checks in with Ruby too, and notes that the week 8 figures will be read with that week in mind.

The decision is not Hannah's. She recommends, with the evidence; the delegate decides, with People Branch's advice. If she ever needs to recommend that probation not be confirmed, NRIO's fictional procedure has People Branch advise how Ruby is told and how she can respond before any decision is made. Nothing in the final review should be news to her.

## When probation is not the right tool

Probation assesses suitability for the duties. It is not a shortcut around other processes, and some concerns belong elsewhere from the start.

```figure
type: decision
title: Is probation the right tool for this concern?
question: What kind of concern is it?
- Possible misconduct, such as misuse of client information | Report it under NRIO's Code of Conduct procedures | Chapter 7; a probation review does not decide whether the Code was breached
- A health, disability or personal issue affecting work | Offer support first, with People Branch advice | Consider adjustments before judging performance
- A gap in training, tools or instructions | Fix the gap first | Probation assesses the person, not the induction
- The employee is not on probation | Use the performance framework | Staff promoted or moved rather than engaged, irregular or intermittent staff, or a waiver (Chapters 5 and 6)
- *Doubt about doing the duties, after support | Record it and discuss it at a probation review | Raised when first seen, with examples and People Branch advice
caption: This is an illustration of how NRIO sorts concerns. Your agency's procedures and People area decide which process applies.
```

One more trap: do not stretch probation to avoid a decision. Whether a period can be extended at all depends on the employee's conditions and the agreement. Ask People Branch early, not in the last week.

## When it goes wrong: the probation nobody reviewed

This is an illustration. At Hannah's briefing for new managers, a People Branch adviser describes a case from another NRIO section in 2025, with names removed. An ongoing APS 4 records officer was engaged on probation. From her third week her manager noticed misfiled documents and missed deadlines. He mentioned them in passing after team meetings, meaning to raise them properly at the first review. That review was postponed twice in a busy quarter, then forgotten. No notes were written. The end date passed with no recommendation, and under NRIO's fictional procedure she was treated as having completed probation.

Four months later the errors were worse, and the manager asked People Branch how to "fail her probation". It was over. The only route left was the performance framework, from the beginning. When the manager raised the concerns formally, the officer said that nobody had told her there was a problem, and the record supported her: there was no written expectation, no feedback note and no review. The formal process took most of the following year. Two colleagues reworked her files throughout, and one asked to move teams.

Nobody acted in bad faith. Each step was put off for a good reason. The cost fell on everyone: on the officer, who never had a fair chance to fix what she did not know was wrong; on the team; and on NRIO, which lost the assessment of suitability that section 44(3) expects to happen during the period itself.

The controls are dull, and they work. Book every review on day one and treat the dates as fixed. Write a short note after each review, share it and keep it. Raise a concern when you first see it, with an example. Send the recommendation before the end date. And if a review slips, tell People Branch that week.

## Exercise: Ruby's probation review plan

This is an illustration. It is Friday 30 October 2026, the end of Ruby's fourth week. In weeks 3 and 4 she handled 240 calls with a total handling time of 2,040 minutes. Marcus sampled 20 of those calls: 15 met the checklist. Of the five that did not, four failed on the case note and one on confirming the caller's identity. Sophie says Ruby is calm with difficult callers and asks good questions. People Branch has given Hannah the end date of Ruby's probation from her conditions.

Write Ruby's probation review plan: the first review's findings against the week 4 expectations, the actions agreed, the next checks and reviews, and what each note records.

### Worked answer

**First review findings.** Average handling time is 2,040 ÷ 240 = 8.5 minutes, within the week 4 expectation of 9 minutes or less. Quality is 15 ÷ 20 = 75 per cent, above the 70 per cent expectation. Ruby is on track, and the note should say so plainly. Four of the five failed calls, 80 per cent of them, failed on case notes, so the development need is specific. The identity check failed once, and that is a privacy matter, so it is fixed at once rather than left for a trend.

**Actions agreed.**

- Marcus goes through the identity steps with Ruby that afternoon. From now on they apply on every call.
- Marcus runs a one-hour session on case notes by Friday 6 November and gives her a one-page note template.
- Sophie checks Ruby's case notes at the end of each day in week 5, from Monday 2 to Friday 6 November.
- Ruby's performance agreement is settled the same day, carrying the same expectations.
- Integrity training stays booked for November, well before the 5 April 2027 limit.

**Next checks and reviews.**

- Week 8 check on Friday 27 November. The expectation is an average of 7.5 minutes or less and at least 85 per cent. Of the 20 calls Marcus samples in weeks 7 and 8, 85 per cent of 20 = 17, so at least 17 must meet the checklist.
- Midpoint review on the date halfway between 5 October and the end date, booked now.
- Final review timed so that Hannah's written recommendation reaches the delegate at least four weeks before the end date, as NRIO's procedure asks.
- The weekly one-to-ones continue in between.

**What each note records.** The date, the evidence, progress against each expectation, what is going well, what needs work with the support agreed, Ruby's own comments and the next date. Ruby receives each note within two working days and adds her comments before it is filed as People Branch directs.

**If the trend turns.** If the week 8 sample falls below 17 of 20 despite the support, Hannah talks to People Branch that week, records the concern and the support offered, and tells Ruby clearly what has to change by the midpoint. She does not wait for the final review.
