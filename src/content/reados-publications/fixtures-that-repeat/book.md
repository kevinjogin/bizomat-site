## About this book

A fixture is a physical agreement about where the workpiece belongs, which loads it can resist and how an operator repeats the set-up. A drawing of a plate and several clamps captures only part of that agreement. The rest lies in contact conditions, tool access, verification, cleaning, wear and the response when something changes.

This book follows a fictional **machined inspection bracket** through fixture development. The blank is nominally 150 mm by 80 mm by 16 mm. A primary support face, a long side and an end provide its starting references. The exercise is to machine a repeatable feature pattern while leaving a method that another competent person can understand and verify.

All dimensions, loads, test results, quantities and financial values are invented for teaching. They are not KEVOS® projects or performance claims. The force examples are simplified static models with explicit assumptions; they do not establish a safe clamp selection, machine program or production approval for a real installation. Manufacturer ratings, actual load envelopes, machine instructions and a competent application review remain necessary.

The ten chapters produce connected outputs: a fixture brief, a contact map, load and deflection checks, an access review, a trial record, an operating method and a maintenance decision. Each exercise includes an answer or an explicit basis for assessing it. Money examples use Australian dollars and exclude GST.

## An original application of established mechanics

The Library already introduces jigs, fixtures, workholding and manufacturing quality. This book concentrates on how to qualify one fixture through a continuous technical case. Its value is in the connections between decisions, including the results that require a design to be changed or a claim to be narrowed.

The supplied handbook informed the topic review. Primary references are cited for the limited principles and equations used. No source drawings, catalogue tables, distinctive examples or chapter structures have been copied. No manufacturer endorses the teaching fixture, and no catalogue part is implicitly approved by appearing in a discussion.

The case concerns stationary machining workholding. Rotating fixtures, lifting devices, pressure equipment, welding distortion and safety-related control systems require additional analysis outside this book. Use the method to ask better questions and record better evidence; adapt the engineering to the actual equipment and consequences.
