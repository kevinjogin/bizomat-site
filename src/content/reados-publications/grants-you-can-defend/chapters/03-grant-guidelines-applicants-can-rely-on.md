Grant opportunity guidelines are a promise to every potential applicant. They say who can apply, what the money is for, how applications will be judged and who will decide. Applicants spend days preparing against that promise. An agency that later judges them by different rules has broken it, whatever it intended.

This is an illustration. In October 2026 Ivy Chen, the Senior Grants Officer, began drafting the guidelines for Round 1 of the Regional Community Facilities Program (RCFP). Chapter 2 settled the program's logic, eligibility and co-contribution. Ivy's task was to turn those decisions into documents a volunteer hall committee could read once and act on. Sam Reid, the Grants Program Manager, reviewed every draft. The Minister approved the guidelines on Tuesday 15 December 2026.

This chapter follows the guidelines from the first draft to the close of the round, at 2:00 pm Canberra time on Thursday 11 March 2027. Assessment is Chapter 5.

## What the CGRPs require of guidelines

The rules on guidelines sit in both parts of the [Commonwealth Grants Rules and Principles 2024](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2024L00854/latest/text) (CGRPs). As Chapter 1 explained, "must" marks a requirement and "should" marks better practice (1.5). Calling a "should" a "must" invents a rule; calling a "must" a "should" hides a breach.

Three requirements apply directly to guidelines.

- **Guidelines for every new opportunity.** Officials must develop guidelines for all new grant opportunities, and revised guidelines where significant changes have been made to one (4.4(a)). Footnote 35 adds that this includes one-off ad hoc grants.
- **Consistency.** Officials must ensure that the guidelines, and any suite of documents that forms them, are consistent with the CGRPs (4.4(c) and 13.4).
- **Publication.** Guidelines "must be made publicly available on GrantConnect", unless there is a specific policy reason not to, for which a minister may seek an exemption from the Minister for Finance (5.2 and 5.8). Any other public information about the opportunity must be "the same, or a subset" of what is on GrantConnect (5.2(a)).

Footnote 36 adds a risk step. Officials who develop guidelines are required to complete a risk assessment, and the Department of Finance, in consultation with the Department of the Prime Minister and Cabinet, agrees the final risk rating. Build that time into the plan.

"Guidelines" means more than one document. The glossary includes application forms, frequently asked questions, draft grant agreements and reporting templates. Everything an applicant reads is part of the promise, and all of it falls under 5.2.

Paragraph 13.3 lists what guidelines should include, "as relevant": objectives, eligibility criteria, weighted assessment criteria, the closing and likely decision dates, the selection process, the decision-maker, the agreement terms, reporting and acquittal, and complaint, review and freedom of information mechanisms. That list is better practice, not a requirement; if you leave an item out, be ready to say why.

## Eligibility criteria and assessment criteria do different jobs

The glossary says selection criteria comprise eligibility criteria and assessment criteria. Eligibility criteria are mandatory. They should be developed to enable objective validation, and they are either "met" or "not met". Assessment criteria are the standards against which applications are judged and, in a competitive round, ranked.

An eligibility criterion is a gate; an assessment criterion is a scale. To sort them, give the same documents to two officers working separately. If they could reach different answers, the question calls for judgement and belongs among the assessment criteria.

This is an illustration. Each RCFP eligibility criterion is settled by a document: the applicant's incorporation or council status, the town's population at the most recent Census, the budget for the grant range and a council's 25 per cent, the project timeline for the 18-month limit, and the time recorded at lodgement.

Two criteria need care. The 18-month test checks only that the plan finishes in time; whether the plan is realistic is judged under criterion 2, capacity to deliver. And because a council's 25 per cent is calculated on eligible project costs, the guidelines list which costs count, so two councils with the same project cannot calculate different contributions.

The opposite mistake is a rule hidden inside a score. If a program will not fund work already under way, say so as an eligibility criterion, not as a reason for assessors to mark projects down.

## Weights and anchors applicants can read

This is an illustration. Each RCFP application is scored from 0 to 10 on three criteria: community need and benefit (40 per cent); capacity to deliver, covering the project plan, budget and risk (30 per cent); and value for money (30 per cent). Assessors score against written anchors, which describe what an application at a given score looks like. An application must score at least 5 on every criterion to be recommended, and those that do are ranked by weighted score.

Paragraph 13.3 asks for clear criteria, how they will be applied and their weighting. Ivy published the anchors as well. An applicant who reads that a 5 on community need requires current local evidence will go and collect it. The anchors for criterion 1 read, in part:

| Score | Anchor for criterion 1, community need and benefit |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | No need is described, or the facility would not be open to the community |
| 5 | The need is shown with some current local evidence, and at least one community group clearly benefits. This is the minimum to be recommended |
| 10 | The need is shown with current local evidence of demand and use, several groups benefit, and the application says how use will be measured |

```figure
type: formula
title: How an RCFP weighted score is calculated
caption: This is an illustration. The weights add to 100 per cent, so the weighted score runs from 0 to 10. The minimum applies before any ranking.
formula: Weighted score = (0.40 × C1) + (0.30 × C2) + (0.30 × C3)
- C1 | Community need and benefit, scored 0 to 10
- C2 | Capacity to deliver, scored 0 to 10
- C3 | Value for money, scored 0 to 10
- Minimum | At least 5 on every criterion
step: Application A scores 8, 6 and 7: 3.2 + 1.8 + 2.1 = 7.1, and every score is at least 5
step: Application B scores 10, 9 and 4: 4.0 + 2.7 + 1.2 = 7.9, but 4 is below the minimum on C3
step: A can be recommended; B cannot, despite its higher weighted score
```

The figure shows why the guidelines need to state the order of the rules. If they said only "ranked by weighted score", B's applicant could fairly expect to rank above A. State the minimum, then the ranking, then how ties are broken.

If the decision-maker may weigh other factors, such as the spread of projects across a region, name them now. Paragraph 13.3 includes "any considerations, prioritisations or other factors that may be taken into account", and 4.7(d) links officials' further recommendations to factors "outlined in the grant opportunity guidelines". A factor that first appears in the brief was never part of the promise.

## Writing for the person filling in the form

Paragraph 13.4 says requirements should be simply expressed and clear in their intent, and that officials should consider testing the guidelines with stakeholders before release. Paragraph 13.5 says the form should help applicants address every selection criterion, and the proposed grant agreement should be included, so applicants know what they would sign.

This is an illustration. Ivy wrote for a volunteer committee working in the evenings. Each eligibility criterion is a yes-or-no question. Each key term, such as "eligible project costs", is defined once. Amounts say "excluding GST", dates give the weekday and times give the time zone. The form follows the criteria in the same order and words.

This is an illustration. In November 2026, Ivy tested the draft with four readers from outside NRIO's region, who could not apply and so gained no early look. Three of the four could not tell which costs counted towards a council's 25 per cent. Ivy added a list of eligible costs and an example: on eligible project costs of A$400,000, a council must contribute at least A$100,000 (25 per cent), so the largest grant it can seek is A$300,000.

## Approval and publication on GrantConnect

Footnote 49 says "GrantConnect is the authoritative source for grant information" and must accurately report approved guidelines. Treat its copy as the master. Every other version, on a website or in a media release, copies it or says less (5.2(a)). Where officials develop a forecast grant opportunity, that must be published on GrantConnect too (5.3).

Paragraph 11.6 says publicly available opportunities should be notified in ways that give all potential grantees a reasonable opportunity to apply. The CGRPs set no minimum open period. Choose one long enough for the slowest legitimate applicant, such as a council whose elected members must commit its contribution.

This is an illustration. The Minister approved the guidelines, the application form and the draft grant agreement together. NRIO held the opening until Monday 1 February 2027, so that the 38 days the round was open did not fall mostly in the summer holidays. Ben Walker, the Grants Officer, published every document on GrantConnect that morning, each with a version number and date. NRIO's own page linked to GrantConnect, and its notices to councils and community networks used the guidelines' words.

## Questions and answers during the round

Frequently asked questions form part of the guidelines, so treat every answer to an applicant as an addition to them. Everyone gets the same answer, on GrantConnect, and no answer can change the guidelines.

This is an illustration. The RCFP guidelines set four rules for questions. They go to one mailbox, and questions taken by phone go into the same log. Ivy approves every answer before release. Ben publishes the week's questions and answers on GrantConnect each Friday, without the asker's name. Questions close at 5:00 pm Canberra time on Thursday 4 March 2027, and the final answers are published on Friday 5 March, six days before the close.

The team does not pre-assess. "Is our project good enough?" gets a pointer to the criteria and anchors, not an opinion, and nobody tells one applicant anything about another. A question passed on by a minister's office, a member of parliament or a senior officer gets the same answer, by the same route, on the same Friday.

Ivy tests each draft answer. If it would change who can apply, what applicants must provide, how they will be judged or when the round closes, it is not an answer. It is a change to the guidelines.

## Changing the guidelines fairly

Under 4.4(a), officials must develop revised guidelines where significant changes have been made to a grant opportunity. Paragraph 13.9 adds better practice. Guidelines should say when criteria may be waived or amended and how any disadvantage will be reduced, and officials should seek ministerial or other appropriate authority before doing so. Guidelines "should not be changed after the grant opportunity is closed to applications". Any departure should be documented and communicated, including whether applicants may revise their applications or a new opportunity will be issued.

A change before the close is best made by addendum: a numbered, dated notice that corrects or changes part of the guidelines, published on GrantConnect beside them. This is an illustration. Under the RCFP guidelines, every addendum needs the Minister's approval, because the Minister approved the guidelines. An addendum that changes what applicants must provide moves the close to at least three weeks after its publication. Any extension applies to everyone and is announced before the original closing time.

An extension for one applicant is a late application by another name. And every day added comes out of the time for assessment and decisions, which Chapter 4 looks at.

## When it goes wrong: a population answer nobody approved

This is an illustration. On Wednesday 17 February 2027, a shire council emailed the RCFP mailbox about a hall in a town of 15,400 people at the most recent Census. A mine had since closed, and the council estimated the town now had 14,700 people. Could it apply?

An officer helping with the mailbox in a busy week replied that afternoon: NRIO would accept a council's own estimate, backed by a council resolution. The officer added the same line to the RCFP page on NRIO's website and, two days later, gave the same answer to a second council by phone. Nobody approved it, and it never reached the question log or GrantConnect, so Ben's Friday check of the log against GrantConnect found nothing.

On Thursday 25 February, the second council asked what form its resolution should take. Ivy had approved no such answer, and she traced it that day. Eligibility had been changed quietly, for two councils, by someone with no authority to change it, and NRIO's website no longer met 5.2(a).

Sam took it to the Director, Regional Programs, that afternoon. Adopting the estimate rule for everyone would need the Minister's approval, change a limit set deliberately in the program's design and, under the three-week rule, push the close back by at least a week. The Director withdrew the answer instead, and told the Branch Manager and the Minister's office in writing. On Friday 26 February the line came off the website and GrantConnect carried a correction. Sam called both councils and apologised, and every step went on the round's file.

The cost was contained: two councils' wasted work, a complaint to the Minister's office and two days of the team's time. Found after the close, it would have been far worse. NRIO would have had to choose between rejecting applications made on its own written advice and accepting them against guidelines that 13.9 says should not change after the close, and either choice would have been unfair to someone.

The control was half built: one mailbox, one approver and weekly publication. Nothing made it bind everyone. Now only Ivy, or Sam in her absence, can release an answer in any channel. Only Ben can edit the RCFP web page, which carries nothing that is not on GrantConnect. Questions about eligibility, criteria, weights or dates go to Sam the same day.

## Exercise: six faults in a draft

This is an illustration. This extract from version 0.3 of Ivy's draft, before Sam's review, contains six faults against the RCFP's settings and the CGRPs; the other clauses are correct. Find each fault, explain it and correct the clause.

| Clause | Draft text |
| --- | --- |
| 2.1 | Incorporated not-for-profit organisations and local councils in NRIO's region may apply. |
| 2.2 | Your facility must be in a town of fewer than 15,000 people at the most recent Census, and your project must have strong community support. |
| 2.3 | Grants of A$50,000 to A$500,000, excluding GST, are available. Every applicant must contribute at least 25 per cent of the grant requested. |
| 3.1 | Criteria: community need and benefit (40 per cent), capacity to deliver (30 per cent), value for money (20 per cent). |
| 3.2 | Applications are ranked by total score, and the highest-ranked are recommended. |
| 4.1 | Applications close at 2:00 pm on Friday 11 March 2027. Late applications will not be accepted. |
| 4.2 | Phone the RCFP team with any question. We will answer you directly and privately. |
| 4.3 | The Minister decides which applications are funded. |

### Worked answer

**Fault 1, clause 2.2: a judgement in an eligibility criterion.** The population test is objective. "Strong community support" is not: two officers could read the same letters and disagree. It belongs to criterion 1. Corrected: "Your facility must be in a town of fewer than 15,000 people at the most recent Census."

**Fault 2, clause 2.3: the co-contribution is wrong twice.** It applies only to councils, and it is calculated on eligible project costs, not the grant. The draft would tell Kellerton Hall Committee Inc., a not-for-profit seeking A$420,000, to find 0.25 × A$420,000 = A$105,000 it does not owe. For the Shire of Eastbrook, with eligible costs of A$800,000, the approved rule requires at least 0.25 × A$800,000 = A$200,000; the draft asks for 0.25 × A$500,000 = A$125,000, or A$75,000 too little. Corrected: "Local councils must contribute at least 25 per cent of eligible project costs. Incorporated not-for-profit organisations need not contribute."

**Fault 3, clause 3.1: the weights add to 90 per cent.** 40 + 30 + 20 = 90. Value for money is weighted 30 per cent, making 100. At 20 per cent the top weighted score would be 9.0, and Application A would score 3.2 + 1.8 + 1.4 = 6.4 instead of 7.1. Corrected: "value for money (30 per cent)".

**Fault 4, clause 3.2: no minimum and no weights.** "Total score" could mean the unweighted sum, which puts Application B (10 + 9 + 4 = 23) above A (8 + 6 + 7 = 21). Under the approved rule, B cannot be recommended at all. Corrected: "To be recommended, an application must score at least 5 out of 10 on every criterion. Those that do are ranked by weighted score and recommended in rank order within the A$10.0 million available." Add how ties are broken.

**Fault 5, clause 4.1: the wrong weekday and no time zone.** 11 March 2027 is a Thursday, as a calendar check confirms. An applicant who trusts "Friday" may plan to lodge a day late. Corrected: "Applications close at 2:00 pm Canberra time on Thursday 11 March 2027." Keep the late rule: the eligibility check will apply it.

**Fault 6, clause 4.2: private answers.** Answers to questions are frequently asked questions, which the glossary counts as part of the guidelines, so they belong on GrantConnect for everyone (5.2). A private phone answer gives one applicant what others lack and leaves no record. Protect privacy by leaving out the asker's name, not by hiding the answer. Corrected: "Email questions to the RCFP mailbox by 5:00 pm Canberra time on Thursday 4 March 2027. Every question and answer is published on GrantConnect each Friday, without the asker's name."

Clauses 2.1 and 4.3 are correct. Clause 4.3 would be better with the likely decision date, which 13.3 lists, but that is a "should": an improvement, not a fault.

Chapter 4 steps back to the decision that came before every one of these clauses: how applicants would be selected at all.
