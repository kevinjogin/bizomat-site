## About this book

Engineers are trained to make things well. They are rarely taught, in the same deliberate way, how to decide well when a finished thing turns out to be less than it should be. That second skill has a long history. Thomas Aquinas, writing in the thirteenth century and building on Aristotle, gave a careful account of practical wisdom, which he called prudence: the habit of reasoning rightly about what to do. His analysis separates the steps of a good decision, names the ways each step fails and insists that a decision is finished only when it is carried out.

This book applies that account to one technical decision, worked through from the first inspection report to a changed procedure. It is written for people who make consequential calls under time pressure: engineers, quality leads, production managers and owners of small technical firms. It assumes no background in philosophy. It assumes you can read a cost estimate and a simple risk matrix.

## How to use the book

Read the chapters in order the first time, because each one picks up the case where the previous one stopped. Every chapter ends with an exercise and a worked answer. Where a BIZOMAT tool performs a calculation used in the chapter, the text links to it and says what it does and does not calculate. The tools give structure; none of them makes the decision.

## The running case

This is an illustration. A small Australian machining workshop has machined 240 cast stainless steel pump flanges for a pump-station contractor at A$240 each, an order worth A$57,600 excluding GST (A$63,360 including GST). Final dye-penetrant inspection of the sealing faces finds indications on 18 flanges. Delivery is due in six working days. The book follows the workshop's decision about those 18 flanges and what it changes afterwards. The workshop, customer, specification, prices, rates and results are invented for teaching. They are not BIZOMAT work, clients or claims.

## Sources and limits

The account of prudence is taken from the *Summa Theologiae* in the public-domain 1920 English translation, cited by part, question and article so you can check every claim. *The Cambridge Companion to Aquinas*, supplied for this book, was read for orientation and is credited; its text, structure and examples are not reproduced. The book uses Aquinas's analysis of practical reasoning and does not argue for or against his theology.

This book is educational. It is not engineering, legal or safety advice. Pressure equipment, water supply, product conformance and workplace safety are regulated, and contracts allocate who may accept a nonconforming product. Real decisions of this kind belong to competent, authorised people working within the applicable law, standards and contract, with qualified review where the consequences warrant it.
