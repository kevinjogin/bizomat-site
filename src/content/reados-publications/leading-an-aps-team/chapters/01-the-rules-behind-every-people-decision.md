A new team leader in the Australian Public Service makes people decisions from the first morning: who answers the phones, whether leave can be approved, what to say when someone asks about pay. Each decision is made under a rule, by someone who holds the power to make it, and any of them may later be reviewed by someone who reads only the record.

This is an illustration. Hannah Lee started at the Northfield Regional Infrastructure Office (NRIO) on Monday 3 August 2026 as its Client Services Manager, an Executive Level 1 (EL1) role. She was promoted from APS 6 in another agency. By Friday 7 August her staff had asked her about rosters, leave, a flexible start time and pay. She knew some of the answers. She did not yet know which questions were hers to answer.

An earlier book in this series, Public Money You Can Account For, gave the team a single chapter. This book gives it ten and follows Hannah through her first year. This chapter builds the map the rest of the book uses: where each rule comes from, who holds each power, and where a manager's authority ends.

## Where the rules come from

People decisions in an APS agency rest on five layers of rules, each made by a different body. Three are on the Federal Register of Legislation. Use the current version of each. The Regulations and Directions replaced older instruments, whose numbering still turns up in old guidance:

- the **Public Service Act 1999**, Compilation No. 22 of 11 December 2024;
- the **Public Service Regulations 2023**, which commenced on 30 March 2023 and replaced the 1999 Regulations;
- the **Australian Public Service Commissioner's Directions 2022**, made by the APS Commissioner, which replaced the 2016 Directions on 1 February 2022. Compilation No. 7 of 1 April 2025 is on the [Register](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2022L00088/latest/text).

Your agency's **enterprise agreement** is approved by the Fair Work Commission. It is not on the Register and differs between agencies, so this book gives no figures from it. **Agency instruments**, the delegations and the written procedures People Branch maintains, come last. Beside all of these sit laws that also bind the Commonwealth: the Fair Work Act 2009, the Work Health and Safety Act 2011 and its Regulations, and the Public Interest Disclosure Act 2013.

## The employer's powers and how they reach you

Section 20(1) of the [Public Service Act](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00538/latest/text) says: "An Agency Head, on behalf of the Commonwealth, has all the rights, duties and powers of an employer" in respect of the agency's APS employees. Section 22(1) adds the power to engage employees. Do not confuse these with section 41, which lists the APS Commissioner's functions.

Section 78(7) lets the Agency Head share the load: "An Agency Head may, in writing, delegate to another person any of the Agency Head's powers or functions under this Act". The written instrument is the source of a delegate's authority. A manager's title does not create one, and neither does custom or a senior officer's email.

This is an illustration. NRIO keeps its people delegations in one instrument, and Hannah reads it in her first week. As an EL1 she can approve leave for her own staff within the enterprise agreement and direct their day-to-day work. The Director engages and promotes staff at APS 1 to 6. Ending employment and deciding suspected breaches of the Code sit with other positions in the schedule. Where Hannah holds no power, she can still gather facts, consult, draft and recommend. That is a real role, but it is not deciding.

## The Values and the Employment Principles

Section 10 of the Act sets six APS Values: Committed to service, Ethical, Respectful, Accountable, Impartial and Stewardship. Stewardship has been in force since 11 December 2024, so guidance listing five Values is out of date. Section 10(6) says the APS "builds its capability and institutional knowledge". For a team leader, that means training people, writing procedures down and planning for the day a key person leaves (Chapter 10).

The Directions say what each Value requires, having regard to a person's duties. Two requirements of the Ethical Value matter to a new manager at once: "following through on commitments made" (s 14(b)) and "reporting and addressing misconduct and other unacceptable behaviour by public servants in a fair, timely and effective way" (s 14(f)). Stewardship requires "ensuring complete, accurate, and appropriately accessible recordkeeping of key actions and decisions" (s 17A(e)).

Section 10A sets the Employment Principles. Among them, the APS makes fair employment decisions with a fair system of review, treats ongoing engagement as the usual basis, "makes decisions relating to engagement and promotion that are based on merit", "requires effective performance from each employee" and "provides workplaces that are free from discrimination, patronage and favouritism" (s 10A(1)(c), (d) and (f)).

These are working rules. Section 10A(2) defines merit in five elements (Chapter 3). Section 50 of the Directions lists what a supervisor does to uphold effective performance, including career conversations at least once a year, a performance agreement for each employee, clear, honest and timely feedback, and prompt action on unsatisfactory performance while "maintaining appropriate records" (Chapters 5 and 6).

## The Code of Conduct, for the team and for you

Section 13 sets the APS Code of Conduct. It binds every APS employee, the manager included. The parts a team leader meets most often say that an employee must:

- "behave honestly and with integrity" (s 13(1)) and "act with care and diligence" (s 13(2));
- "treat everyone with respect and courtesy, and without harassment" (s 13(3));
- "comply with any lawful and reasonable direction given by someone in the employee's Agency who has authority to give the direction" (s 13(5));
- take reasonable steps to avoid any conflict of interest, real or apparent, and disclose material personal interests (s 13(7)).

Subsection 13(5) cuts both ways. A direction binds staff only when it is lawful, reasonable and given by someone with authority. "Take the phones on Monday morning" usually meets the test. "Stay back every night until the queue clears" may not: the Fair Work Act allows a full-time employee to be asked to work more than 38 hours a week only if the additional hours are reasonable (s 62), and long hours are a safety question too (Chapter 8).

Section 15(3) requires the Agency Head to establish written procedures for determining whether an employee has breached the Code and what sanction, if any, to impose. They must meet the basic procedural requirements in the Directions and have due regard to procedural fairness. This is an illustration. Under NRIO's procedures a manager reports a suspected breach, and neither investigates nor decides it (Chapter 7).

## Enterprise agreements and agency procedures

Many questions in a manager's first month are answered by the enterprise agreement: pay points, salary advancement, leave, flexible hours and working from home. The length of probation comes from the agreement or the agency's conditions of engagement. Read the clauses your team asks about most. When a team habit differs from the agreement, follow the agreement. When the agreement is unclear, ask before you answer.

Some agency procedures exist because the law requires them: the Code procedures under section 15(3), and performance policies and processes that the Agency Head ensures are "fair, open and effective" and "used to guide salary movement" (Directions s 49(1)(a)(iv) and (g)).

This is an illustration. NRIO's procedures for recruitment, performance and conduct belong to People Branch, and the versions in this book are fictional. In her first week Hannah reads them, her delegations and the agreement's contents page, and asks her People Branch adviser where each kind of question should go.

## Safety, fair work and disclosure laws

The Work Health and Safety Act 2011 binds the Commonwealth (s 10), and its regulator is Comcare (s 4). A person conducting a business or undertaking "must ensure, so far as is reasonably practicable, the health and safety of" its workers (s 19(1)), and health means "physical and psychological health" (s 4). Every worker must take reasonable care for their own health and safety and that of others (s 28).

Officers must exercise due diligence (s 27). For the Commonwealth, an officer is a person who "makes, or participates in making, decisions that affect the whole, or a substantial part" of its business or undertaking (s 247(1)). That turns on the role, so check with your WHS area instead of guessing. Sections 47 to 49 require consultation with workers, and the psychosocial risk rules in regulations 55A to 55D of the WHS Regulations have applied since 1 April 2023 (Chapter 8).

The Fair Work Act 2009 applies to APS employment too. Besides the National Employment Standards, enterprise agreements and the limit on hours, it gives the right to request flexible working arrangements (ss 65 to 65C, Chapter 9).

Public interest disclosures are made under the Public Interest Disclosure Act 2013; section 16 of the Public Service Act was repealed from 15 January 2014. A discloser is protected, including from disciplinary action for making the disclosure (PID Act s 10(1)(a)), as Chapter 7 explains.

## Your authority and the People Branch's

Every people decision can then be sorted by four questions. What is the decision? Which rule governs it? Who holds the power? What record will show it was made properly?

This is an illustration. Hannah's answers fall into four groups:

- **Decide:** day-to-day directions about work, rosters and priorities; feedback; her staff's performance agreements; leave within her delegation.
- **Recommend:** engagement and promotion, as a panel member or chair; the outcome of probation; resources for the section.
- **Report:** suspected breaches of the Code, safety incidents and hazards, and anything that may be a public interest disclosure.
- **Ask:** agreement questions she is unsure of, and anything with legal consequences, such as ending someone's employment.

People Branch owns the procedures, advises on them, runs formal processes and keeps the official records. It does not manage the team. A manager who sends every question to People Branch is not managing; one who answers every question alone is guessing.

## When it goes wrong: a pay rise promised in week two

This is an illustration. In Hannah's second week, Leila Farah asks about pay. She has heard that salary movement at NRIO depends on the end-of-year rating. After a hard year on complaints, can she count on moving up?

Here is how that goes wrong. Hannah, keen to back a strong team leader, says: "You've earned it. You'll get your increment; I'll make sure of it." Leila mentions it to Marcus, who wonders whether his own pay will be "sorted out" the same way.

The promise fails on every count. Salary movement is set by the enterprise agreement and guided by the agency's performance policies and processes (Directions s 49(1)(g)). It depends on the agreement's rules and on an assessment at the end of a cycle that has barely begun. At NRIO the Director moderates ratings across the branch, so even the rating is not Hannah's alone. And by answering now, Hannah has prejudged an assessment that must be fair and consistent across the team.

Whatever happens next, the promise does damage. If no move follows, Leila has been misled by a manager she has known for a fortnight. If one does, others can fairly ask whether it was settled in August, in a workplace that should be free from "patronage and favouritism". Either way Hannah has made a commitment she cannot keep.

What Hannah actually said was smaller and better. Leila's work would be assessed against her performance agreement at the end of the cycle, the same way as everyone's, and the enterprise agreement set how salary moves. Hannah offered to agree specific expectations now, so Leila would know what a strong year looks like (Chapter 5), and to show her the agreement's clause by Friday, after checking it with People Branch. Then she wrote a three-line file note.

The control is a habit, not a form. Before answering any request about pay, promotion, hours, leave or conditions, find the rule and the decision-maker. If the answer is not yours to give, say so, say who decides and say when you will come back. "I'll find out and tell you by Friday" is a commitment a new manager can keep.

## Exercise: six people decisions

This is an illustration. Six people decisions will reach Hannah over the coming year. For each, name the main source of authority, who decides, and what Hannah herself does.

| | Decision | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Moving Sophie from email to the phones for the Monday morning peak | August 2026 |
| B | Whether Ruby's engagement continues at the end of her probation | End of her probation |
| C | Who fills the APS 5 position Daniel leaves | From November 2026 |
| D | Responding to Mei's written request for flexible working arrangements | January 2027 |
| E | Leila's report that Aaron opened a client record without a work reason | Late November 2026 |
| F | A caller threatens Sophie on the phone | Monday 16 November 2026 |

### Worked answer

**A: Hannah or Marcus decides.** The source is the Agency Head's employer powers (s 20(1)), used through supervisors, and Sophie's duty to follow a lawful and reasonable direction from someone with authority (s 13(5)), within the hours the enterprise agreement sets. Hannah explains the reason, the Monday peak (Chapter 2), asks Sophie about any constraint and records the roster. If a change could affect health or safety, she consults the people affected (WHS Act s 49(d)).

**B: a delegate decides; Hannah recommends.** Probation is a condition of engagement (s 22(6)(a)). The Directions require the Agency Head to ensure that a new employee, other than one engaged for irregular or intermittent duties, is engaged on probation unless that is waived, and that processes are in place to assess the employee's suitability (s 44). Hannah sets clear expectations, reviews Ruby's work, keeps records and recommends to the delegate, with People Branch advice (Chapter 4).

**C: the Director decides on a panel's recommendation.** The source is the power to engage and promote (ss 20 and 22), limited by merit (s 10A) and by sections 24 and 25 of the Directions. At NRIO the Director is the delegate for APS 1 to 6, and People Branch runs the process. Hannah helps define the duties, may chair the panel, declares any interest (s 13(7)) and documents the assessment (Chapter 3).

**D: NRIO as employer decides, through the person its procedure names, who may be Hannah.** An eligible employee may request flexible working arrangements in writing (Fair Work Act s 65), and the employer must respond in writing within 21 days (s 65A). A refusal must rest on reasonable business grounds, after genuinely trying to reach agreement and considering the consequences for the employee, and must set out the effect of sections 65B and 65C. Your enterprise agreement may add to this. Before 4 January, Hannah checks who signs the response. Then she meets Mei, prepares the reasons and keeps to the time limit (Chapter 9).

**E: an independent decision-maker decides; Hannah reports.** The source is the Code (s 13) and NRIO's procedures under section 15(3), which require a decision-maker who "is, and appears to be, independent and unbiased" (Directions s 61). Reporting is part of the Ethical Value (s 14(f)). Hannah reports promptly to People Branch, keeps the matter confidential, and neither interviews Aaron nor gathers evidence herself (Chapter 7).

**F: Hannah acts at once; NRIO settles the lasting controls.** The WHS Act requires health and safety, psychological health included, to be ensured so far as is reasonably practicable (ss 4 and 19), and every worker has duties (s 28). Hannah makes sure Sophie is safe and supported and reports the incident under NRIO's procedures. Lasting controls are settled with the WHS area and the Director after consulting the team (ss 47 to 49; Chapter 8).

Only A, and D if NRIO's procedure names her, are decided within the section. In B and C she recommends; in E she reports and stays out; in F she acts first, then hands the lasting decisions to those who hold them.
