Break-even answers a limited but essential question: what sales level is needed to cover the costs included in the model? It is a threshold, not a growth strategy, cash forecast or adequate owner-income target.

## Contribution margin
If a product sells for $100 excluding GST and costs that vary with the sale are $40, contribution is $60. The contribution margin ratio is **$60 ÷ $100 = 60%**. That contribution pays fixed expenses and then contributes to profit.

The usual simplified formula is:

**Break-even revenue = fixed costs ÷ contribution margin ratio.**

For fixed monthly costs of $12,000 and a contribution margin of 60%, break-even revenue is **$12,000 ÷ 0.60 = $20,000**. At that level, variable costs are $8,000, fixed costs are $12,000 and simplified operating profit is zero.

## Units and sales mix
If contribution per unit is $30 and fixed expenses are $9,000, break-even volume is **300 units**. Round up when the result is not a whole unit. For a multi-product business, calculate an appropriate weighted contribution margin using a defensible expected sales mix; if the mix changes, the threshold changes.

## Price discount warning
Suppose an offer sells for $100 with $40 variable cost. Contribution is $60. A discount to $90 with unchanged variable cost cuts contribution to $50, a **16.7% reduction in contribution per sale**, even though the selling price fell by only 10%. You would need 20% more unit sales to generate the same total contribution, assuming costs and mix stay unchanged.

## Capacity also matters
A business cannot sell infinite volume. Consider delivery hours, machine capacity, replenishment time and service quality. An offer with high theoretical margin may still be unattractive if it blocks more valuable work.

## Build a pricing decision sheet
For every offer record **price ex GST | direct variable cost | contribution | capacity used | expected demand | payment terms | returns and rework risk**. Compare a base and alternative price at the same service level. Avoid calculating a margin on GST-inclusive receipts where GST is being remitted.

## Profit target versus break-even
To approximate revenue required for a target operating profit, add that profit target to fixed costs and divide by the contribution ratio. For a target of $3,000 per month with $12,000 fixed expenses and a 60% contribution ratio, required revenue is **($12,000 + $3,000) ÷ 0.60 = $25,000**. Additional tax, finance and owner drawings require separate planning.

## Exercise
Calculate one break-even scenario using real recent expenses. Then stress-test it at contribution margins five percentage points lower and higher. Record which costs are excluded so the result is not mistaken for total cash sufficiency.

*All examples are simplified illustrations, not a substitute for cost accounting or pricing validation.*
