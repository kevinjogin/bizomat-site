# Keyword strategy and opportunities

The registry is `seo/keyword-registry.json`, generated by `node scripts/seo/keywords.mjs` from the seed list in that script. It maps each seed to **one** canonical page and records the reason. The generator fails if a target is not an indexable canonical page. This file also serves as the keyword opportunities summary.

## How scores work

| Component | Weight | Source today |
|---|---:|---|
| Business relevance | 30% | Judged: how directly the query leads to KEVOS work |
| Search demand evidence | 20% | **UNKNOWN** until Search Console, Bing or an authorised research source supplies it |
| Existing impressions and opportunity | 20% | **UNKNOWN** until Search Console is connected |
| Competitive feasibility | 15% | **UNKNOWN**; no competitor metrics are invented |
| Content quality and readiness | 15% | Judged from the actual target page |

Unknown components are excluded from the weighted average, not counted as zero, and every score is marked `provisional: true` with the share of weight it rests on (currently 45%). Scores will change once real data arrives; treat the current ranking as a starting order, not a forecast.

## What the mapping shows

1. **Commercial intent is concentrated on one page.** Fourteen commercial seeds point to `/services/`, a single page with anchors for Engineering and CAD, Manufacturing Support, Operations Improvement and Project Support. One page cannot be the best answer for "jig and fixture design", "SolidWorks drafting services" and "lean manufacturing consulting" at once. This is the largest structural opportunity, and it needs owner-approved copy for each service: deliverables, process, typical engagements and proof that already exists on the site. No suburb pages and no invented case studies.
2. **Calculator intent now has real landing pages.** Thirty seeds map to individual WORKOS tool pages built in this work, each with method, worked example and limitations. These are the most likely early sources of organic traffic, and international discovery is realistic for them.
3. **Educational intent is well covered by READOS** for fixtures, cycle time and productivity. Two gaps are confirmed: an engineering RFQ guide and an engineering commissioning-documentation checklist. They are briefs only (below); nothing is generated or published without review.
4. **Branded intent** maps to the home page, WORKOS and READOS. MAKEOS has no page of its own; `/tools/?collection=makeos` is a filtered view, not an indexable page.

## Priority opportunities (provisional order)

1. **Service pages for core offers** (review and copy needed): jig and fixture design, Engineering and CAD (SolidWorks), manufacturing support, operations improvement, project support, each linking to its WORKOS tools and READOS books.
2. **Extend ten thin tool guides**, starting with OEE (quick), Takt Time and Reorder Point. Once complete, they qualify for pages automatically.
3. **Link tools and books both ways**: for example the Jig and Fixture ROI tool and *Fixtures That Repeat*, the cutting-speed tool and *Machining by the Numbers*, the tolerance-stack tool and *Fits That Work*, the welding heat-input tool and *Weld Repairs That Hold*.
4. **Editorial briefs** for the two confirmed gaps (below).
5. **Measure before rewriting.** Once Search Console has four or more weeks of data, the weekly engine proposes title and description changes only where CTR is well below the site's own median for the same position band.

## Editorial briefs (drafts require expert review before publishing)

- **How to prepare an engineering RFQ.** Audience: small manufacturers buying engineering, tooling or fabrication. Content: scope statement, drawings and revision control, tolerances and inspection requirements, quantities and schedule, commercial terms, how to compare quotes. Link to the Manufacturing Quote Builder and Supplier Comparison tools and to *Win Work You Can Deliver*. No invented supplier examples.
- **Engineering project commissioning documentation checklist.** Audience: project and maintenance engineers. Content: pre-commissioning checks, test records, punch lists, operating and maintenance manuals, training and handover sign-off. State clearly that site-specific regulations and standards govern; avoid implying certification.
