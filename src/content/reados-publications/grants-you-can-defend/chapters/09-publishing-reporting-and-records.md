A grant becomes public twice. The first time is on GrantConnect, where anyone can see who received money, how much and for what. The second is when someone opens the file: an auditor, a complaints officer, a person using freedom of information law, or the officer who inherits the program. Both records have to be right, and both have to be there.

This is an illustration. In June 2027 NRIO's Regional Programs Branch began executing the Round 1 grant agreements for the Regional Community Facilities Program (RCFP), the first on Tuesday 15 June. Each agreement started a clock. Ben Walker publishes on GrantConnect, Nadia Haddad keeps the records and Ivy Chen checks both. Sam Reid, the Grants Program Manager, wants every grant file to answer its own questions long after its decision-makers have moved on.

## What GrantConnect must show

GrantConnect is the Australian Government's whole-of-government grants information system, as the glossary of the [Commonwealth Grants Rules and Principles 2024](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2024L00854/latest/text) (CGRPs) describes it. Section 5 of the CGRPs sets out what goes on it, and each item is a "must":

- grant opportunity guidelines, unless there is a specific policy reason not to publish them (5.2);
- forecast grant opportunities, where officials prepare them (5.3);
- information on each grant and on later variations, within 21 calendar days (5.4);
- where a minister approves the grants, information on the grants awarded and the decisions, as soon as practicable (5.5).

The award record must include a link to the published guidelines, where they are available (5.4). The CGRPs do not list the other fields: GrantConnect's forms set them, and footnote 50 points to Finance's guidance on grants reporting. Use the current versions, not a colleague's memory of last year's.

Agencies may publish grant information in other ways too, but footnote 49 says GrantConnect "is the authoritative source for grant information and must accurately report approved grant opportunity guidelines and details of grants awarded." A media release may round a figure; the GrantConnect record has to be exact.

## Counting twenty-one calendar days

Paragraph 5.4 requires the information "no later than twenty-one calendar days after the grant agreement or the variation for the grant takes effect". Three phrases do the work.

"Calendar days" means weekends and public holidays count. The last day is the date of effect plus 21 days.

"Takes effect" is not always the day of signing. Footnote 52 says the date of effect can be the date the agreement is signed or a specified starting date, and where there is no agreement the information must be published as soon as practicable. Read the commencement clause before you set the due date.

"Or the variation" means each reportable variation has its own clock.

This is an illustration. RCFP agreements take effect when the second party signs (Chapter 7). Kellerton Hall's, the first, took effect on Tuesday 15 June 2027, so it had to be on GrantConnect by Tuesday 6 July 2027, which is 15 June plus 21 days. NRIO's practice now has four parts, added after the failure described below. Nadia enters the date of effect and the due date in the branch's publication register on the day of execution. Ben publishes within five working days. Ivy reads the register every Monday. And Nadia is trained to publish when Ben is away.

## Variations and corrections

Not every variation goes on GrantConnect. Footnote 51 points to Finance's guidance on reportable variations and gives examples: significant additional payments of relevant money, extensions into a new financial year, novation (moving the agreement to a different grantee) and changes in organisation details. Minor variations, such as indexation, changes to project or research team members, or date changes within the same financial year, do not need to be reported.

For dates, the test is the financial year, not how long the delay felt. Moving a report's due date from December to January stays within one financial year, so it is minor. Moving a completion date from April to August crosses 30 June, so it is reportable. Reportable or not, every variation needs approval by the right delegate and a record on the file (Chapter 8).

Footnote 49's "must accurately report" covers mistakes as well as gaps.

This is an illustration. In August 2027 Nadia's monthly reconciliation compared GrantConnect with the branch's grants register and the finance system. Brindlemere Football Netball Club's grant appeared on GrantConnect as A$301,000; the agreement says A$310,000. Ben corrected the record that day. The grant file shows what was wrong, what it was changed to, when, by whom and how the error was found.

## When less than everything can be published

Sometimes full publication would conflict with the Privacy Act 1988, another statute or a grant agreement's terms. Officials then "must publish as much information as legally possible", for example by leaving out a grantee's name and personal information but reporting the other details (5.7(a)). They must document the reasons for not reporting fully (5.7(b)). They should also take all reasonable steps so that future agreements do not prevent disclosure (5.7(c)). Paragraph 5.6 adds a quieter requirement: officials must identify whether a grant agreement contains confidentiality provisions.

Not publishing at all is a different matter, and officials cannot decide it. Under 5.8 a minister may seek an exemption from the Minister for Finance where publishing could adversely affect the achievement of government policy outcomes, or there is a specific policy reason not to publish. The responsible minister must write to the Minister for Finance with the rationale, and officials should consult Finance before the process starts.

This is an illustration. RCFP's agreement template has no clause that prevents publication, and Ben checks each signed agreement for confidentiality provisions, as 5.6 requires, noting the result on the file. One draft award record described a project by naming a volunteer and the street where she lives. Ben removed both, published the rest, and filed a note of what was left out and why.

## Recording the Minister's decisions

Because the Minister approved every RCFP grant, paragraph 5.5 applies: NRIO must record the grants awarded and the decisions on GrantConnect as soon as practicable. Paragraph 4.13 adds that decisions reported to the Minister for Finance under 4.11(a), for grants in a minister's own electorate, and 4.12(a), for grants officials recommended be rejected, must also be recorded on GrantConnect "with a summary of the basis of the approval". Chapter 6 covered the reports to the Minister for Finance and their tabling; GrantConnect is the public face of the same decisions.

This is an illustration. Two of the 31 grants were approved against officials' recommendations, and one of them, Corran Bay Progress Association's hall kitchen, is in the Minister's own electorate. Ben takes each summary of the basis from the Minister's written reasons, not from the brief or a conversation with the Minister's office, and files the published text with the decision record. If the summary and the recorded reasons ever differ, someone will ask which is wrong.

Other reporting, such as an agency's annual report or answers to the Parliament, has its own rules, outside this book. Ask your governance area what applies, and make sure every figure you supply can be traced to the file.

## Keeping the full file

Publication shows the outcome; the file shows how it was reached. Paragraph 3.15 of the CGRPs says: "Officials must retain appropriate records, consistent with their accountability obligations, and ensure the records are available on request." Its footnote points to Part 2-3 of the PGPA Act and the Archives Act 1983.

Section 24 of the [Archives Act 1983](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A02796/latest/text) says a person must not engage in conduct that results in the destruction or other disposal of a Commonwealth record, the transfer of its custody or ownership, or damage to or alteration of it. The penalty is 20 penalty units. The exceptions in subsection 24(2) include what a law requires, what the National Archives of Australia permits or a practice it has approved, and a normal administrative practice it has not disapproved. A record can be in any form, including electronic, and subsection 24(5) treats a change that stops information being obtained from an electronic record as destruction. An email recording a grant decision deserves the same care as a signed form. Before deleting anything about a grant, ask your records area which approved practices apply.

Several requirements create specific records:

- the approving official must record in writing the basis for the approval (4.5);
- ministers must record the basis for approval and their reasons for departing from recommendations (4.10);
- decision-makers must document the reasons for decisions in awarding grants (14.7);
- the approval of each commitment of relevant money must be recorded in writing (section 18 of the PGPA Rule);
- where reporting is not full, the reasons must be documented (5.7(b)).

Others are better practice, such as documenting why a non-competitive process was used (11.5, a "should").

This is an illustration. NRIO keeps one master file for the round: the guidelines and their approval, the assessment plan, Hartley Probity Services' written advice, the panel minutes, the brief and the Minister's decision record. Each grant file holds what is specific to that grant and cross-refers to the master file instead of copying it, so each shared document has one authoritative version.

## A file that survives scrutiny

A file is tested by people who were not there. An auditor asks whether the rules were followed and whether the records prove it. A person who makes a freedom of information request receives documents, not explanations, so the documents have to explain themselves. A complainant, often an unsuccessful applicant, asks whether they were treated fairly, and the answer has to come from the record.

One test serves all three. Could a capable stranger say, from the file alone, what was decided, by whom, when, on what basis and what happened next? Wherever they would have to ask a person, the file has a gap.

Four habits close most gaps:

- record decisions when they are made, with the true date;
- save emails and messages that make or record a decision to the grant file, the same week;
- write a file note of any call or meeting that decides something (Chapter 6 showed why);
- correct a record by adding to it, never by changing or deleting what was there.

## When it goes wrong: three late grants and a decision in an inbox

This is an illustration. Three RCFP agreements were executed and took effect on Friday 18 June 2027, in the busiest fortnight of the round. Their GrantConnect deadline was Friday 9 July 2027. Ben meant to publish them before starting three weeks of leave on Monday 28 June, but publication was on his own task list, not in a shared register, and nobody else in the branch had published on GrantConnect. He returned on Monday 19 July and published all three on Tuesday 20 July, 32 days after the agreements took effect and 11 days late.

Meanwhile an unsuccessful applicant from the town of one of the three projects searched GrantConnect, found nothing, and wrote to the Minister's office that a grant had been made "in secret". The record was late, not secret, but showing that took three days, because a decision about that grant was not on the file. The grantee had asked to start work a month later than planned, and the Branch Manager had agreed by email. The email was in Ivy's inbox and the reasons in a reply in Ben's. Until both were found, nobody could show who had approved the change, or why.

The cost was a missed "must" under 5.4, reported to the CFO area under NRIO's compliance procedure, a complaint to the Minister's office, three days of staff time, and a grantee whose agreement and approval email gave different start dates.

The controls are those now in NRIO's practice: a team-owned publication register with due dates entered on the day of execution, a second trained publisher, a weekly check, and a rule that any email deciding something about a grant goes to the grant file the same week, with a note of the reasons if the email does not give them.

## Exercise: audit a grant file

This is an illustration. In May 2029, before the evaluation starts, Sam asks Ivy to audit one closed grant file against the checklist above. The grantee, an incorporated community association not named elsewhere, received A$180,000, excluding GST, for a hall kitchen and an accessible entry. RCFP pays 40, 40 and 20 per cent: on execution, on an approved mid-point report and on an approved final report and acquittal.

| No. | What the file holds |
|---|---|
| 1 | Eligibility check, both assessors' scores and reasons, moderation note |
| 2 | Declarations of interests from the panel and the first assessor; a note says the second assessor's is "on the shared drive" |
| 3 | Cross-reference to the brief (Fri 7 May 2027) and the Minister's decision record (Mon 31 May 2027) on the master file |
| 4 | Agreement executed and in effect Thu 17 Jun 2027, signed for NRIO by the Branch Manager; section 18 record |
| 5 | An email from the association's treasurer giving its bank account; nothing else on bank details |
| 6 | GrantConnect award record published Thu 24 Jun 2027, linked to the guidelines |
| 7 | Payments: A$72,000 on Tue 22 Jun 2027; A$72,000 after the approved mid-point report; A$36,000 after the final report was approved on Tue 17 Apr 2029 |
| 8 | Variation 1: mid-point report due date moved from Wed 15 Dec 2027 to Fri 14 Jan 2028. A file note says the Branch Manager approved it by email; the email is not filed |
| 9 | Variation 2: completion date moved from Fri 28 Apr 2028 to Thu 31 Aug 2028, signed by the Branch Manager, in effect Wed 5 Apr 2028. No GrantConnect record |
| 10 | A site visit note: "Visited. All good." No date and no author |

Say whether each item passes. For each fault, name the rule or procedure it breaks, say whether it is a "must", a "should" or NRIO's own procedure, and give the fix.

### Worked answer

This is an illustration.

1. **Passes.** The assessment record is complete.
2. **Fails.** Officials must disclose material personal interests (3.12) and must retain appropriate records (3.15). A note that a declaration is somewhere else does not show that it exists. Fix: find it and file it. If it cannot be found, record that, ask the assessor to state in writing whether they had any interest at the time, and tell the Director.
3. **Passes.** One master copy, cross-referenced, is better than a second copy that can drift.
4. **Passes.** A$180,000 is within the Branch Manager's A$500,000 delegation, and the section 18 record is there.
5. **Fails.** NRIO's procedure requires a call-back to confirm bank details before the first payment (Chapter 7). It is one of the controls through which NRIO meets the "must" in section 10 of the PGPA Rule, and an emailed bank account is a common route for redirecting payments. Fix: ask the CFO area whether a call-back was recorded elsewhere. If not, report the control failure and confirm with the association that it received all three payments.
6. **Passes.** 17 June plus 21 days is 8 July 2027. The record went up on 24 June, day 7, with the link to the guidelines.
7. **Passes.** 40 per cent of A$180,000 is A$72,000 and 20 per cent is A$36,000; A$72,000 + A$72,000 + A$36,000 = A$180,000. The first payment came after the agreement took effect, and the last after the final report was approved.
8. **Fails in part.** 15 December 2027 and 14 January 2028 both fall in 2027–28, so this is a date change within one financial year and does not go on GrantConnect. But the approval is not on the file, which falls short of 3.15 and breaks NRIO's rule on emails. Fix: file the Branch Manager's email.
9. **Fails.** 28 April 2028 falls in 2027–28 and 31 August 2028 in 2028–29, so this is an extension into a new financial year and is reportable under 5.4. It took effect on 5 April 2028, so the deadline was Wednesday 26 April 2028. The new date is within the 18-month limit (17 June 2027 plus 18 months is 17 December 2028). Fix: publish the variation now, note on the file that it is late and why, report the missed deadline under NRIO's compliance procedure, and check the other grant files for the same gap.
10. **Fails.** An undated, unsigned note proves nothing, and NRIO's procedure requires dated, signed notes. Fix: ask the officer who visited to write a note from their calendar and travel records, dated May 2029 and marked as written then. Never backdate it.

Five items pass, one fails in part and four fail. Three faults (items 2, 8 and 9) fall short of a "must" in the CGRPs, and two (items 5 and 10) break NRIO's own procedures. Item 9 is a missed public deadline and item 5 a failed fraud control, so both are reported. The rest are for the file's owner to fix this month.

A complete, dated file is also the raw material for the last task in a program's life, and Chapter 10 turns to it: finding out whether the program worked.
