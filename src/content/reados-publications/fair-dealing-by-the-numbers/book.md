## About this book

Most small businesses want to deal fairly. Fewer have a clear idea of what fair dealing requires when the details get difficult: when a customer is desperate, when an estimate is wrong, when a defect is invisible, when there is not enough capacity to go round, or when an invoice turns out to be too high. Good intentions do not answer those questions. A method does.

Thomas Aquinas, writing in the thirteenth century, set out one of the most careful accounts of justice in buying, selling and sharing that we have. He distinguished fairness between two parties to an exchange from fairness in distributing something among many, gave each its own arithmetic, and examined in detail what a seller may charge, what must be disclosed, and how a wrong is put right. This book applies that account to the everyday commercial life of an Australian fabrication workshop. It assumes no background in philosophy, only a willingness to work through the numbers.

## How to use the book

The eight chapters follow one job from enquiry to final invoice. Read them in order the first time. Each chapter works through the case, includes at least one error and how it was found and corrected, and ends with an exercise and a worked answer. Where a BIZOMAT tool performs a calculation the chapter uses, the text links to it and says exactly what it calculates and what it leaves to you.

## The running case

This is an illustration. A small fabrication workshop is asked by a food manufacturer to quote a 3,000-litre stainless steel holding tank for a salted product. Over the course of the job the workshop costs and prices it, discovers an error in its description of the tank, finds and repairs a weld defect, shares a fortnight of welding capacity among three customers, overcharges for a variation, and has to answer a request for a delivery date it cannot meet. The workshop, its customers, specifications, rates and prices are invented for teaching. They are not BIZOMAT work, clients or claims. Amounts are in Australian dollars and exclude GST unless stated; GST is 10%.

## Sources and limits

Aquinas is cited from the *Summa Theologiae* in the public-domain 1920 English translation, by part, question and article, so every claim can be checked. *The Cambridge Companion to Aquinas*, supplied for this book, was read for orientation and is credited; its text, structure and examples are not reproduced. The book uses Aquinas's analysis of justice and does not argue for or against his theology.

This book is educational and general. It is not legal, tax, financial or engineering advice. The Australian Consumer Law, tax law and contract law apply to real transactions, and their application depends on the facts. For specific situations, consult the ACCC, the ATO, your state fair trading agency or a qualified adviser, and have food-contact and pressure equipment designed and approved by competent people.
