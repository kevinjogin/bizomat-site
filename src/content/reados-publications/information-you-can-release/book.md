## About this book

Government information belongs to the public in a particular way. People have a legal right to ask for documents, a legal right to have their personal information handled properly and a legal expectation that the records of decisions will be kept. Each right comes with time limits, tests and review. An officer who knows how the laws fit together can release what should be released, protect what must be protected and explain the difference in writing.

This book follows one information access and privacy team through a busy year. It starts with how the information laws fit together and with keeping records that can be found, then moves through handling a freedom of information request, exemptions and the public interest test, consulting third parties, decisions, reasons and review, the privacy principles in daily work, notifiable data breaches, publishing proactively and a team process that meets its deadlines. Each chapter ends with an exercise and a worked answer.

The chapters use **the Information Access and Privacy Team of the Northfield Regional Infrastructure Office**, a fictional agency, as a continuous example. Its staff, requests, documents, applicants, breach and decisions are invented for teaching. They are not a real agency, request or person, a BIZOMAT project or a claim about how any agency works. The team's own procedures, such as its triage rules and internal targets, are the fictional agency's choices and not legal requirements.

The Information Commissioner issues guidelines on freedom of information and on the Australian Privacy Principles. They are not legislative instruments, and this book does not quote them. Read the current versions on the Commissioner's website alongside it.

## An independent treatment

This is general education, not legal advice, and not an official publication of any agency or of the Information Commissioner. Law and guidance change, and every agency has its own authorisations, procedures and records authorities. Before you act, check the version of each law in force and your agency's own instructions, and ask your FOI, privacy, legal or records area when the consequences matter.
