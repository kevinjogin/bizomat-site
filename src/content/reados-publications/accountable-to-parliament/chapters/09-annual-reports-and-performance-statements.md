Most accountability work answers someone else's questions. The annual report is different: once a year the agency tells the Parliament, in its own words and from its own records, what it set out to do and what it achieved. Senators, committees and auditors then read it beside everything else the agency has said. A result that cannot be traced to its records, or that quietly measures something new, will be found.

This is an illustration. It is July 2027. The 2026–27 reporting period ended on Wednesday 30 June, and NRIO's annual report must reach the Minister by Friday 15 October 2027. NRIO's planning and performance team compiles it; Clare Whitfield's unit writes the account of the year's external scrutiny and reads the whole report against the parliamentary record. Sam Reid supplies the results for the Regional Community Facilities Program (RCFP), the program Senators questioned at Estimates in June.

## The performance framework in the PGPA Act

Part 2-3 of the [Public Governance, Performance and Accountability Act 2013](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2013A00123/latest/text) (the PGPA Act) sets a cycle of duties for the accountable authority, who for NRIO is the Chief Executive Officer (CEO). Each is a "must".

- **Corporate plan:** prepare one "at least once each reporting period" (s 35(1)(a)). Unless the rules say otherwise, a reporting period is the 12 months from 1 July (s 8).
- **Records:** "cause records to be kept that properly record and explain the entity's performance in achieving its purposes" (s 37(1)).
- **Measurement:** measure and assess that performance (s 38(1)).
- **Performance statements:** prepare them "as soon as practicable after the end of each reporting period" and include them in the annual report tabled in the Parliament (s 39(1)).
- **Annual report:** "prepare and give an annual report to the entity's responsible Minister, for presentation to the Parliament" (s 46(1)).

The plan says in advance what will be measured and how, the records hold the evidence, and the statements report against the plan. A link that fails early cannot be repaired at the end: if records were not kept during the year, no drafting in September will produce a result someone else can check.


## The corporate plan fixes the measures in advance

The PGPA Rule 2014 (the Rule) sets the contents of a corporate plan. Under section 16E it covers "at least 4 reporting periods": the year it is prepared for and the three after. It must set out the entity's purposes, its key activities and its operating context. For each year it covers, it must also specify performance measures that meet section 16EA, with a target for each one where setting a target is reasonably practicable. The plan goes to the responsible Minister and the Finance Minister before it is published (s 16E(5)). It must be published on the entity's website "by the last day of the second month of the reporting period" (s 16E(3)): for a year that starts on 1 July, 31 August.

Section 16EA sets tests for those measures, among them reliable and verifiable sources and an unbiased basis for assessment. For an annual report, the plan's most important job is to fix each measure's definition before anyone knows the results: what is counted, from which source, by what calculation, against what target. That definition is the standard the results will be held to.

This is an illustration. NRIO's 2026–27 corporate plan, published by Monday 31 August 2026, set two RCFP measures for the year:

- **RCFP-1:** Round 1 grant decisions made by 30 June 2027. Target: decisions by that date.
- **RCFP-2:** the share of eligible Round 1 applications assessed against the published criteria. Target: 100 per cent.

Both are output measures. The program's outcome measures, designed with the program, first appear with targets in the 2027–28 corporate plan, published by Tuesday 31 August 2027: the hours a week each funded facility is booked, the community groups using it regularly, and the days it is lost to its condition. Their results can come only after the works finish and a year of use is recorded.

Sometimes a definition has to change. If the plan is varied during the year and the accountable authority considers the variation significant, section 16E(6) applies the section to the varied plan, which must be published as soon as practicable. Once the year has ended, the definition for that year is fixed. The statements must report on the method the plan set out; any other basis should be shown separately, labelled, with the reason.

## Records that explain the result

Section 37 asks for records that "properly record and explain" performance, kept in a way that enables the annual performance statements to be prepared (s 37(2)(b)). The responsible Minister and the Finance Minister are entitled to full and free access to them (s 37(3)).

"Explain" is the demanding word. Explaining a result needs the definition, the source data, the calculation, who did it, who checked it and any judgement made along the way. The test: could an outsider recalculate the result from the file without asking anyone? The outsider may be an auditor. The Auditor-General can audit annual performance statements (Auditor-General Act s 15), on request by the responsible Minister or the Finance Minister (PGPA Act s 40), and the appropriateness of an entity's performance measures and its reporting against them (Auditor-General Act s 18A).

This is an illustration. For each 2026–27 result, NRIO keeps a short evidence file: the method card from the corporate plan, the source data, the calculation, and who calculated and who checked it, with dates. For RCFP-1 the source is the Minister's decision record of 31 May 2027; for RCFP-2, the eligibility record and the Round 1 assessment report.

## What the annual performance statements must contain

Section 39(2) of the Act says the statements must provide information about the entity's performance in achieving its purposes, and must comply with the rules. Section 16F of the [PGPA Rule](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2014L00911/latest/text) supplies those rules.

Section 16F(1) is easy to overlook. In preparing the statements, the accountable authority must measure and assess performance in accordance with the method set out in the corporate plan, and in any Portfolio Budget Statement or other portfolio estimates statement, prepared for that year. The statements test the plan. They are not a fresh choice of what to show.

Section 16F(2) then lists three matters the statements must include:

- **Statements.** These include the accountable authority's opinion that they "accurately present the entity's performance in the reporting period" and comply with section 39(2).
- **Results.** The results of measuring and assessing the entity's performance in achieving its purposes.
- **Analysis.** The factors that may have contributed to performance, including any changes to the entity's purposes, activities or capability, or to its environment, that may have had a significant impact.

The accountable authority signs that opinion. At NRIO that is the CEO, so every officer who supplies a result supplies part of what the CEO certifies.

The statements must report every measure in the plan, including the misses (s 16F(1) and (2)). Good statements put each result next to its target, say plainly whether it was met, and show earlier years on the same definition. When a target is missed, say so first, then give the reason and what the agency is doing about it. A miss explained honestly costs far less than one someone else discovers.

## The annual report and its required contents

Section 46(3) of the Act says the annual report must comply with the rules, which the Joint Committee of Public Accounts and Audit (JCPAA) must approve on behalf of the Parliament (s 46(4)). For non-corporate Commonwealth entities such as NRIO, they are sections 17AA to 17AJ of the Rule, added in 2016 and amended since. Older guidance refers to the "Requirements for Annual Reports" issued by the Department of the Prime Minister and Cabinet. They are obsolete.

Section 17AB says the report "must comply with the guidelines for presenting documents to the Parliament", which sit outside legislation; read the current version. Section 17AC(2) says information in the report "must be relevant, reliable, concise, understandable and balanced". Section 17AD lists what the report must include.

```figure
type: checklist
title: What a non-corporate entity's annual report must include
caption: PGPA Rule s 17AD. Most items have their own section setting out the detail, and the list of requirements in Schedule 2 to the Rule is one of the aids to access.
- A review by the accountable authority | of the entity for the period (s 17AD(a))
- An overview of the entity | role, functions, structure, outcomes and programs, purposes, accountable authority (s 17AE)
- A report on performance | the annual performance statements (s 16F) and a report on financial performance (s 17AF)
- Management and accountability | governance, the audit committee, external scrutiny, people, purchasing and contracts (s 17AG)
- Contract and remuneration details | organisations paid under reportable contracts (s 17AGA); executive remuneration (Subdivision C)
- The annual financial statements | as required by PGPA Act s 43(4) (s 17AD(e))
- Other mandatory information | including information correcting the record in an earlier report (s 17AH)
- A letter of transmittal and aids to access | signed and dated by the accountable authority (s 17AI); contents, index, glossary and contacts (s 17AJ)
```

Two items matter most to a parliamentary unit. Section 17AG(3) requires information on "the most significant developments during the period in external scrutiny of the entity, and the entity's response to that scrutiny", including reports by the Auditor-General, parliamentary committees and the Commonwealth Ombudsman. And if a previous annual report contains a significant statement of fact that has "proved to be wrong in a material respect", the new report must include "information correcting the record" (s 17AH(1)(e)).

Once the report is presented to the Parliament, it must be published as soon as practicable using the digital reporting tool administered by the Finance Minister (s 17ABA).

This is an illustration. Clare's draft of the 2026–27 external scrutiny section covers the Budget estimates hearing on Wednesday 2 June 2027 and the CEO's letter of Friday 4 June correcting evidence given there (Chapter 10). The inquiry, the Ombudsman's investigation and the ANAO audit begin after 30 June 2027, so they belong in the 2027–28 report. A hearing is not one of the reports the section lists, but a corrected statement about a grants round is among the year's most significant developments.

## Two deadlines: to the Minister, then to the Parliament

Section 46(2) of the PGPA Act says the annual report must be given to the responsible Minister by "the 15th day of the fourth month after the end of the reporting period", or the end of any extension granted under section 34C(5) of the [Acts Interpretation Act 1901](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C1901A00002/latest/text). For a year ending on 30 June, that is 15 October.

Tabling is a separate step. Section 46 sets no period for presenting the report to the Parliament, so section 34C(3) of the Acts Interpretation Act applies: the Minister must have a copy laid before each House "within 15 sitting days of that House after the day on which he or she receives the report". That date depends on when each House sits. Do not say that every entity must table its annual report by 15 October. A few reports have a tabling date in their own Act, such as the ANAO's annual report (Auditor-General Act s 28(2)(a)), but that is not the general rule.

If the report cannot be given on time, the accountable authority may apply to the Minister for an extension, but only within the specified period, with a written statement explaining why compliance will not be reasonably possible (s 34C(4)). The Minister may grant a reasonable extension (s 34C(5)) and must table the statement and the reasons within 3 sitting days of each House (s 34C(6)(a)). A report that is simply late needs a written explanation to the Minister not later than 14 days after the end of the period (s 34C(7)): for a report due on Friday 15 October 2027, by Friday 29 October 2027. That explanation is tabled too.

The letter of transmittal, signed by the accountable authority, is dated on the day the accountable authority approves the final text for printing (Rule s 17AI).

## When it goes wrong: the on-time measure that moved

This is an illustration. NRIO's 2025–26 corporate plan included a measure for its facility works: the share of works projects due that year that finished by the completion date in their first approved project plan. The target was 80 per cent. In 2024–25, 13 of 20 projects did: 65.0 per cent.

During 2025–26, the area managing the works began counting a project as on time if it finished by its completion date as last varied. Nobody varied the corporate plan or told the team preparing the statements. On the new count, 26 of 28 projects finished on time: 92.9 per cent. The 2025–26 statements reported that figure, "up from 65.0 per cent", and the analysis credited better project monitoring. The CEO signed the opinion that the statements accurately presented NRIO's performance, and the report was tabled.

On the method in the plan, 18 of the 28 projects finished by their first approved date: 64.3 per cent. A percentage point is the difference between two percentages. Performance had not improved by 27.9 percentage points (92.9 − 65.0). It had slipped by 0.7 of a point (64.3 − 65.0).

The problem surfaced in July 2027, when the planning and performance team first checked each area's method card against the corporate plan. The card said "as last varied"; the plan said "first approved". By then the cost was fixed. The Parliament had a tabled report, certified as accurate, showing an improvement that had not happened. The Minister's office had quoted the figure in a media release in late 2026, and NRIO had to tell the office it could not be relied on; what to do about the release was the Minister's decision (Chapter 10). And NRIO judged the 2025–26 statement significant and wrong in a material respect, so the 2026–27 report must include information correcting the record (s 17AH(1)(e)).

The 2026–27 statements report the measure on the published method for all three years: 65.0 per cent, 64.3 per cent and, for 2026–27, 21 of 30 projects, or 70.0 per cent. The target was not met in any year, and the statements say so.

Three controls would have caught it. The method card is fixed with the corporate plan, and any change needs a recorded decision. A changed definition is built into a varied plan or disclosed in the statements, with results on both bases. And before the CEO signs, someone who did not prepare a result checks its method card against the plan. NRIO's audit committee, whose functions must include reviewing the appropriateness of performance reporting (Rule s 17(2)(b)), now receives the method cards too.

## Exercise: draft the RCFP section

This is an illustration. It is August 2027. Draft the RCFP section of NRIO's 2026–27 annual performance statements. Use the two measures in the 2026–27 corporate plan and these facts from the settled facts sheet (Chapter 3) and the evidence files:

- 186 applications were received; 9 were ineligible and were not assessed; 177 were assessed against the published criteria.
- On Monday 31 May 2027 the Minister approved 31 grants worth A$9.98 million, excluding GST, 2 of them against officials' recommendations.
- No funded works had finished by 30 June 2027.
- The CEO's evidence at Estimates on 2 June 2027, that all 186 applications were assessed, was corrected by letter on 4 June 2027.

Give each result with its calculation and whether the target was met, a short analysis, and a note on the outcome measures.

### Worked answer

This is an illustration. One version of the section follows.

**Regional Community Facilities Program (RCFP)**

| Measure (2026–27 corporate plan) | Target | Result | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| RCFP-1: Round 1 grant decisions made by 30 June 2027 | By 30 June 2027 | Decisions made on 31 May 2027 | Met |
| RCFP-2: Share of eligible Round 1 applications assessed against the published criteria | 100% | 177 of 177 eligible applications (100%) | Met |

**Analysis.** Round 1 received 186 applications. All were checked for eligibility; 9 (4.8 per cent) were ineligible and were not assessed, and the other 177 were assessed against the published criteria. The Minister made the decisions on 31 May 2027, 30 days before the target date: 31 grants worth A$9.98 million, excluding GST, including 2 that officials had not recommended. These figures are consistent with NRIO's evidence to the Senate committee as corrected on 4 June 2027 (see External scrutiny).

**Outcome measures.** The program's three outcome measures, on use, regular user groups and days lost to a facility's condition, begin in the 2027–28 corporate plan. No funded works had finished by 30 June 2027, so there are no outcome results for 2026–27. Each funded project's baseline was supplied with its application.

The arithmetic behind it:

| Step | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---:|
| Applications assessed | 186 − 9 | 177 |
| RCFP-2 result | 177 ÷ 177 | 100% |
| Share of applications ineligible | 9 ÷ 186 = 0.0484 | 4.8% |
| Days between decisions and target date | 31 May to 30 June 2027 | 30 days |

The draft holds up for five reasons.

1. **It uses the plan's definitions.** RCFP-2 counts eligible applications, so its base is 177. "186 of 186" would repeat the error the CEO corrected. "177 of 186, or 95.2 per cent" (177 ÷ 186 = 0.952) would measure something the plan did not define and seem to miss a target that was met.
2. **It claims no outcomes.** Two output targets met show the round ran on time and on its rules, not that any facility is better. The section does not write "31 communities will have better facilities".
3. **It states the hard fact neutrally.** Readers will look for the 2 decisions against officials' recommendations, so the section states them once, without adjectives or the Minister's reasons, which are the Minister's to give (Chapter 2).
4. **It matches the record:** the settled facts sheet, the answers to Q03 and Q04 (Chapter 3) and the correcting letter.
5. **Each result has its evidence file,** from which an auditor could recalculate it (s 37).

Chapter 10 turns to the letter that corrected the CEO's evidence: how NRIO found the error, who had to correct it, and how.
