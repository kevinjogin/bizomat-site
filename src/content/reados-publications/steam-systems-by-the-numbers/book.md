## About this book

Steam is the most common way factories move large amounts of heat. Food plants cook, sterilise and clean with it; laundries, breweries, hospitals and chemical plants depend on it. It carries a great deal of heat in a small mass, gives it up at a steady temperature, and can be piped across a site. It is also easy to waste. Steam leaks, failed traps, flash steam vented to the sky and hot condensate run to drain can cost a plant more than the process uses well, and steam plant carries risks, of burns and of stored energy, that demand respect.

This book works steam through by numbers. It starts with the steam table and what sensible and latent heat mean, then covers the boiler and the cost of a tonne of steam, the steam a process needs, pressure reduction, pipe sizing, steam traps, flash steam and condensate recovery, and finally inspection, safety and the regulations that apply. Each chapter works one question through the running case, shows a failure and how it was found, and ends with an exercise and its worked answer.

## The running case

The book follows **SV-8**, the steam system of a fictional food factory. A gas-fired shell boiler raises up to **1,000 kg/h** of saturated steam at **800 kPa absolute**, about 7 bar gauge, from feedwater at **80 °C**. A 60 m steam main carries it to the cooking area, where a pressure reducing station drops it to **400 kPa absolute**, about 3 bar gauge, for four jacketed cooking kettles, each rated at **100 kW**. Condensate from the kettles is collected and pumped back to the boiler's feed tank; about **70%** of the steam raised returns as condensate. The plant runs about **4,000 hours** a year.

All quantities, prices and results are invented for teaching. This is an illustration. None of it describes a KEVOS® client, supplier or project. Heat is costed at an assumed gas price of **A$22 per gigajoule** and a boiler efficiency of 85%. Money is in Australian dollars, excluding GST.

## How to use the book

Chapter 1 is the foundation: read it first. Chapters 2 and 3 cover making and using steam, Chapters 4 and 5 moving it, Chapters 6 and 7 getting the condensate back, and Chapter 8 keeping the plant safe and inspected. The companion book *Heat Transfer by the Numbers* covers insulation and heat exchangers, which steam systems use throughout.

## Source limits and professional responsibility

The supplied thermodynamics notes informed the range of subjects. Steam properties are taken from a published saturated steam table and quoted as given; other values chosen for the case are labelled. Boilers and pressure equipment are regulated plant in Australia: their design, registration, operation, inspection and maintenance are governed by work health and safety law and the applicable standards, and real systems must be designed, operated and maintained by competent, appropriately qualified people. Steam and condensate cause severe burns. This book is educational and is not a design or operating manual.
