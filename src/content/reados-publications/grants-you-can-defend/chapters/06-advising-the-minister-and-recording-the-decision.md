A grant decision made by a minister rests on two documents: the advice officials gave and the record of what the minister decided. If either is missing or vague, the decision cannot be explained later, however sound it was. The CGRPs set out what each must contain, and what must be reported when a decision departs from the advice or falls in the minister's own electorate.

This is an illustration. On Friday 7 May 2027 the Branch Manager, Regional Programs, signed the brief that took Round 1's ranked list to the Minister. Sam Reid drafted it with Ivy Chen and Ben Walker from the assessment record (Chapter 5). On Monday 31 May 2027 the Minister approved 31 grants totalling A$9.98 million. They were 29 of the 31 projects officials recommended, and 2 that officials had assessed as meeting the minimum but ranked below the funding line. One of those two, Corran Bay Progress Association Inc., is in the Minister's own electorate.

## What the written advice must contain

Two duties sit behind the brief. Officials must advise a minister who is considering a grant on the relevant requirements of the PGPA Act, the PGPA Rule and the CGRPs (4.4(d) and 3.13). And where a minister approves grants, officials must give written advice (4.6). Under 4.6 of the [CGRPs](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2024L00854/latest/text), that advice must, at a minimum, say explicitly that the proposal is a "grant"; set out the requirements that apply, particularly the minister's recording and reporting obligations, and the legal authority; outline the selection process and criteria; and "provide clear advice on the merits", relative to the guidelines and value with relevant money.

Written means written: the minister must receive written advice on the merits before approving a grant (4.10(a)). A conversation with an adviser in the minister's office can add to that advice, but it cannot replace it.

## Categories, ranks and the funding line

When officials brief a minister on the merits of applications, paragraph 4.7 adds three requirements. Officials must sort applications into three categories: those that fully meet the selection criteria, those that partially meet them, and those that do not meet any. They may add rankings. They must indicate which applications can be supported within the available funding. And they "must recommend that applications that do not meet any of the selection criteria be rejected" (4.7(c)). Any other specific recommendations are a "should" (4.7(d)).

This is an illustration. NRIO applied the categories to the assessment criteria, a choice the brief explained, using the guidelines' minimum. An application scoring at least 5 on every criterion fully meets the criteria, one scoring at least 5 on some but not all partially meets them, and one scoring below 5 on all three meets none. Of the 177 applications assessed, 61 fully met the criteria, 104 partially met them and 12 met none. The nine ineligible applications were never scored, so the brief listed them separately, each with the eligibility criterion it failed, and recommended their rejection.

Attachment A ranked all 177 applications with their criterion scores, weighted score, grant sought, running total and federal electorate. The funding line fell after the 31st:

| Rank | Applicant | Weighted score | Grant sought | Running total | Recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 30 | Not-for-profit, unnamed | 6.80 | A$125,000 | A$9.77m | Approve |
| 31 | Not-for-profit, unnamed | 6.75 | A$150,000 | A$9.92m | Approve |
| 32 | Not-for-profit, unnamed | 6.70 | A$240,000 | A$10.16m, over | Reject: beyond the funding |
| 34 | Yarrowmere Showground Committee Inc. | 6.60 | A$260,000 | n/a | Reject: beyond the funding |
| 38 | Corran Bay Progress Association Inc. | 6.35 | A$140,000 | n/a | Reject: beyond the funding |

The 31 recommended projects left A$80,000 unallocated. The next application in rank order needed A$240,000, so the line fell there.

Two choices made the brief stronger than the minimum. First, it recommended a decision on every application, in the CGRPs' own words: approve the 31, and reject the other 155 (146 assessed and 9 ineligible). For the 30 below the line that fully met the criteria, it said they could not be supported within the available funding. Nobody later has to argue about whether a decision went against advice. Second, it showed each project's electorate, so the Minister's office could see which decisions would bring reporting obligations. None of the 31 recommended projects is in the Minister's electorate.

## The approval under section 71

The Minister approves the spending under section 71 of the [PGPA Act](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2013A00123/latest/text). A minister must not approve a proposed expenditure of relevant money unless "satisfied, after making reasonable inquiries, that the expenditure would be a proper use of relevant money" (s 71(1)). Having approved it, the minister must "record the terms of the approval in writing as soon as practicable after giving the approval" (s 71(2)(a)).

The brief is most of the Minister's reasonable inquiries. For grants, paragraph 4.10 adds that the minister must record in writing:

- the basis for the approval, relative to the guidelines and value with relevant money;
- the basis for not approving any grant that officials recommended be approved;
- the basis for approving any grant that officials recommended be rejected;
- any conflicts of interest relating to the decision, which must also be declared as appropriate.

Decision-makers must also document the reasons for decisions in awarding grants (14.7).

This is an illustration. NRIO attached a decision sheet to the brief: every application with its recommendation, a box for the decision and a space for reasons, then a line for conflicts of interest and space to sign and date. Signed, with the approved grantees and amounts, it records the terms of the approval for section 71 and the basis for each decision for 4.10. Officials prepare the form; the reasons must be the Minister's own.

## The decision and its record

This is an illustration. The decision sheet came back signed on Monday 31 May 2027. The Minister approved 29 of the 31 recommended projects. The two not approved were the 22nd-ranked, a council project for A$215,000, and the 30th-ranked, for A$125,000. The Minister also approved two applications that officials had recommended be rejected: Yarrowmere Showground Committee Inc.'s pavilion upgrade (ranked 34th, A$260,000) and Corran Bay Progress Association Inc.'s community hall kitchen (ranked 38th, A$140,000). A short handwritten reason sat beside each change.

Check the total before anything goes out:

- recommended: A$9,920,000;
- less the two not approved: A$215,000 + A$125,000 = A$340,000;
- plus the two approved against advice: A$260,000 + A$140,000 = A$400,000;
- approved: A$9,920,000 − A$340,000 + A$400,000 = A$9,980,000, across 31 grants and within the A$10.0 million.

Sam's team then checks the record, not the decision: a reason for every departure, each related to the guidelines and value with relevant money, the conflicts line completed, and exact names and amounts. Any gap goes back to the Minister's office in writing before the announcement, with the requirement it serves. Officials neither judge the Minister's reasons nor fill gaps with words of their own.

The money is committed later, when the Branch Manager, as delegate, enters into each grant agreement (Chapter 7).

## Decisions against advice and in the Minister's electorate

The CGRPs allow both. "Ministers may approve grants within their own electorate" (4.11), and ministers "may approve grants that are not recommended by officials" (4.12). Paragraph 11.7 adds that those involved should be aware that the geographic spread of grants may be seen as a sign of a round's general equity, and of party-political bias.

- **Against advice.** A minister must report to the Minister for Finance every approval of a grant that the official "recommended be rejected". The report "must include a brief statement of reasons" and must go "as soon as practicable following the approval" (4.12(a)). Footnote 46 adds that December decisions should be reported before 31 January.
- **In the minister's own electorate.** The minister must write to the Minister for Finance advising of the details (4.11(a)). A copy of the letter to the grantee is enough, except where the grant was also approved against advice; with no such letter, the minister must write as soon as practicable after the decision (4.11(b)). A decision against advice in the minister's own electorate must be included when writing under 4.11 (4.12(b), for members of the House of Representatives). Two kinds of grant are exempt (4.11(c)): those senators approve in their own state or territory, and those awarded by formula.
- **Afterwards.** The Minister for Finance must table these reports in each House "as soon as practicable after the end of each quarter" (4.13). Quarters start on 1 January, 1 April, 1 July and 1 October (footnote 47). The decisions must also be recorded on GrantConnect with a summary of the basis of the approval (4.13), which the entity must do as soon as practicable (5.5).

## Telling applicants and giving feedback

This is an illustration. When the decisions are announced in June, NRIO writes to all 186 applicants on the same day. The 31 successful applicants receive an offer and the draft grant agreement (Chapter 7). The other 155 are told which stage the application reached (ineligible, below the minimum, or above the minimum but not funded) and how to ask for feedback. Nobody should learn the outcome from a media release first, or be told anything before the Minister has decided.

The CGRPs say feedback to unsuccessful applicants "promotes transparency in decision-making" and builds their capacity to apply in future, and that the best method depends on the program and its resources (13.5). This is an illustration. NRIO offers each unsuccessful applicant a written summary of its own moderated scores and reasons, and a short call with Ivy or Ben. Feedback is also the cheapest way to improve Round 2's applications.

Feedback comes from the record, not from memory. Give the applicant its own scores, reasons and rank, never another applicant's scores or its assessors' names, and nothing about the Minister's reasons beyond the summary on GrantConnect.

## Handling complaints

The CGRPs say guidelines should describe "complaint handling, review and/or FOI mechanisms" (13.3). Complaints are normal in a competitive round, and how they are handled is part of the record.

This is an illustration. In July 2027 the applicant ranked 32nd complained: it had narrowly missed the line, then watched two lower-ranked applications receive grants. NRIO's complaints coordinator, who had no part in the round, handled it under the process the guidelines describe and gave a date for a full reply. The reply explained that the Minister is the decision-maker and may approve grants officials did not recommend, and that such decisions are reported to the Minister for Finance, tabled and published on GrantConnect with a summary of the basis. It gave the applicant its own scores and reasons and set out the review options. It did not apologise for the decision, guess at the Minister's reasons or promise anything for Round 2.

## When it goes wrong: advice by phone, never recorded

This is an illustration. On Wednesday 19 May 2027, twelve days after the brief went up, the Minister's adviser rang Sam. The Minister was considering Yarrowmere and Corran Bay. What would it take to fund them, and were there problems with either? Sam answered from memory. Both met the minimum on every criterion. Funding both needed A$400,000 against A$80,000 unallocated, so about A$320,000 of recommended projects would have to come out. Both approvals would have to be recorded and reported, and Corran Bay reported as an own-electorate grant. He added one detail: Yarrowmere's A$300,000 project relied on A$40,000 from another funder that was not yet confirmed. Nobody wrote a file note, and no email followed.

In July, when the 32nd-ranked applicant complained, the Branch Manager asked what advice officials had given on the two approvals against advice. The file held the brief of 7 May and the signed decision sheet, and nothing in between. Sam remembered the call clearly; the adviser did not recall the funding point. Neither could show what had been said.

The only written advice on Yarrowmere's merits was a line of scores in Attachment A, silent on the unconfirmed funding. If that funding fell through, the project would be A$40,000 short, and nobody could show that the Minister had been told. Sam wrote a file note that day, dated the day he wrote it and saying when the call took place. The Branch Manager sent the office a written advice setting out what officials had said, and Ivy asked Yarrowmere to confirm its other funding.

Every question from a minister's office about a pending decision gets a written answer, the same day or the next, filed with the brief. A phone call gets a file note the same day: who called, when, what was asked, what was said and what was promised. And the brief should give a short merit summary for the applications just below the funding line, the likeliest to be approved against advice. If it was worth saying to a minister's office, it is worth writing down.

## Exercise: the record and reports for two approvals against advice

This is an illustration. The decision sheet signed on Monday 31 May 2027 shows the following for the two applications approved against officials' recommendation. The conflicts line is blank.

| Item | Yarrowmere Showground Committee Inc. | Corran Bay Progress Association Inc. |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Project and grant | Pavilion upgrade, A$260,000 | Community hall kitchen, A$140,000 |
| Rank and weighted score | 34th, 6.60 | 38th, 6.35 |
| Brief's recommendation | Reject: fully meets the criteria but cannot be supported within the available funding | The same |
| Location | Not in the Minister's electorate | In the Minister's own electorate |
| Minister's annotation | "Approved. No other approved project serves this district." | "Approved. The hall is the town's relief centre when floods cut the road; it needs a working kitchen to feed residents." |

Prepare the decision record and the reporting for both. Say what is complete and what officials must ask the office for. Then list every report or publication required: who makes it, to whom, what it contains and by when.

### Worked answer

This is an illustration.

**The decision record.** Each approval needs the terms (grantee, project and amount), signed and dated, for section 71(2)(a); the basis for approving a grant officials recommended be rejected (4.10(d)); the basis relative to the guidelines and value with relevant money (4.10(b)); and any conflicts of interest, recorded and declared as appropriate (4.10(e)).

- **Terms.** Complete: the signed sheet names both grantees, projects and amounts, dated 31 May 2027.
- **Corran Bay's basis.** The annotation ties the approval to community need and benefit, the first criterion in the guidelines, weighted 40 per cent, but says nothing about value with relevant money, which 4.10(b) also asks for.
- **Yarrowmere's basis.** The annotation says no other approved project serves the district, but not how that relates to the guidelines or value with relevant money.
- **Asking the office.** For both, officials ask the office, in writing, whether the Minister wishes to complete the basis. They do not draft the reason.
- **Conflicts.** The line is blank for both. A nil declaration is still a record, so officials ask the office to complete it (4.10(e)).

**Reports and publication.**

| Who | To whom | What | When |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The Minister (NRIO drafts) | Minister for Finance | Both approvals against advice, each with a brief statement of reasons (4.12(a)) | As soon as practicable; NRIO's target is a draft within a week |
| The Minister, same letter | Minister for Finance | Corran Bay's details as an own-electorate grant (4.11(a)), including the approval against advice (4.12(b)); a grantee letter copy is not enough (4.11(b)) | The same letter |
| Minister for Finance | Each House | A copy of the report (4.13) | As soon as practicable after the quarter; received in June, tabled after 30 June 2027 |
| NRIO | GrantConnect | Both decisions, with a summary of the basis of each (4.13, 5.5) | As soon as practicable |
| NRIO | GrantConnect | Each grant, with a link to the guidelines (5.4) | Within 21 calendar days after its agreement takes effect: one taking effect on Tue 15 Jun 2027, by Tue 6 Jul 2027 |

The brief statement of reasons uses the Minister's recorded reasons and nothing else. For Corran Bay the draft reads: "Approved because the hall is the town's relief centre when floods cut the road, and it needs a working kitchen to feed residents." Both wait for the office's answers. Speed matters too: a letter that reaches Finance in June is tabled after the June quarter; one that slips into July waits a quarter longer.

The GrantConnect summaries and the tabled report will be read side by side, so they should use the same words, taken from the signed record. File the letter, the office's replies and the GrantConnect entries with the brief and the decision sheet. The decisions were the Minister's to make; the record lets NRIO show that each was made, recorded and reported as the CGRPs require.
