## About this book

Public servants work for the government of the day, and the government answers to Parliament. In practice that means officials answer Senators' questions at Estimates, draft answers to questions on notice, appear before committees, open their records to the Auditor-General and the Ombudsman and report each year on what their programs achieved. Each of these has its own rules and its own risks. An official who knows how they work can give accurate, timely and consistent answers, and can fix a mistake before it grows.

This book follows one parliamentary and accountability team through a demanding year. It starts with how accountability works and who answers for what, then moves through Senate Estimates briefs, questions on notice, committee inquiries, ANAO performance audits, implementing audit recommendations, internal audit and the audit committee, the Commonwealth Ombudsman, annual reports and performance statements, and finally correcting the record. Each chapter ends with an exercise and a worked answer.

The chapters use **the Parliamentary and Accountability Unit of the Northfield Regional Infrastructure Office**, a fictional agency, as a continuous example. Its staff, hearings, questions, inquiry, audit, complaint and reports are invented for teaching. They are not a real agency, committee, audit or person, a BIZOMAT project or a claim about how any agency or committee works. Hearing dates and deadlines in the case are fictional.

Much of parliamentary practice sits outside legislation: in the standing orders and orders of each House, in their resolutions and in government guidelines for official witnesses. This book describes those practices in general terms and does not quote them. Read the current versions published by the Parliament and the Department of the Prime Minister and Cabinet alongside it.

## An independent treatment

This is general education, not legal or procedural advice, and not an official publication of any agency, committee or office-holder. Law, orders and guidelines change, and every agency has its own procedures for parliamentary work, audit and complaints. Before you act, check the version of each rule in force and your agency's own instructions, and ask your parliamentary, legal or audit area when the consequences matter.
