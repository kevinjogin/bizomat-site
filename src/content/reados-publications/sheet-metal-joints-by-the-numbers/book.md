## About this book

Most sheet metal products are held together by joints that involve no heat: rivets, clinches, self-clinching nuts and studs, screws and folded seams. They are quick, they keep coatings intact, they join metals that cannot be welded together, and they are easy to get wrong in ways that only show in service. A rivet strong enough on its data sheet can tear out of sheet that is too thin or too close to the edge; a thread tapped in 1.2 mm steel holds far less than the screw it carries; a stainless rivet in an aluminium door can corrode the door around it.

This book works through those joints with numbers. It starts with what each joint in a product must do, then covers how riveted joints fail and how to check them, blind and solid rivets, pitch and edge distance, clinching and self-piercing rivets, self-clinching fasteners, screws and threads in thin sheet, folded lock seams, galvanic corrosion and sealing, and testing and costing joints.

It suits mechanical designers, drafters and fabrication engineers. It assumes basic statics and stress, and some familiarity with sheet metal parts; *Sheet Metal Parts by the Numbers* covers the folded parts themselves, *Brazing and Soldering by the Numbers* the joints made with filler metal, and the Library’s resistance welding book the spot weld. This book stays with the mechanical joints.

## The running case

Every chapter follows one fictional product. This is an illustration. A fabricator makes EN-700, an outdoor equipment enclosure 700 mm high, 500 mm wide and 250 mm deep. Its body is 1.2 mm galvanised steel, folded and joined; its door is 2.0 mm aluminium sheet on a riveted hinge; inside, a 25 kg equipment plate hangs from two brackets fixed to the back wall. The enclosure is built in batches and travels to site by road.

The product, the fabricator and the numbers are invented; none describes BIZOMAT work or a real product. Material strengths and fastener ratings are labelled illustrations: real values come from the material's certificate and the fastener maker's published data. Prices are in Australian dollars, excluding GST.

## Source limits and professional responsibility

The calculations in this book use standard strength-of-materials methods with stated simplifications, and every calculation was checked by running it. Joint design for structural, lifting, pressure or safety-critical duties is governed by the applicable Australian Standards, the fastener maker's data and the product's own requirements, and must be checked by a competent engineer. This book is educational; it does not replace a qualified design check or the testing a real joint requires.
