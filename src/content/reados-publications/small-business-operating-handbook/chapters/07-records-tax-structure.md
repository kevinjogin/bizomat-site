A sustainable business must know what it is legally, what records it must keep and which payments are obligations rather than discretionary costs. Administrative gaps often appear only when cash is tight, making them harder to resolve.

## Structure affects risk and administration
Common Australian operating structures include sole trader, partnership, company and trusts used in different arrangements. These differ in legal identity, personal liability, tax, control, obligations and costs. A company is not simply a larger sole trader, and a trust is not the same legal arrangement as a company. Seek professional advice before formation, restructuring or transferring assets or intellectual property.

Review the structure at material changes: taking investors, hiring, acquiring another business, transferring licences, taking debt or preparing a sale. Changing structure may have costs and consequences.

## Keep an evidence trail
Maintain dated invoices, receipts, bank statements, contracts, payroll records where relevant, tax correspondence and asset registers. Store private files with limited access, sensible naming and backups. Reconciling monthly is easier than reconstructing decisions at tax time.

## Taxes and GST
If registered for GST, some cash received may include amounts to be accounted for through Business Activity Statements, subject to input tax credits and the entity's circumstances. This cash is not automatically free to spend. A separate tax provision or account can help prevent unplanned shortfalls, but amounts must be based on actual obligations.

Confirm GST registration thresholds, accounting basis, record retention periods, deductible treatment and due dates with current Australian Taxation Office guidance. Never substitute a generic percentage of turnover for an accurate calculation.

## Employing people
Consider wages, PAYG withholding, superannuation, leave, workers compensation, payroll systems and payroll tax where applicable. Employee classification and award obligations require proper review. Rules and commencement dates may change; consult current ATO, Fair Work and state revenue guidance before implementing payroll changes. Do not rely on an old presentation for compliance deadlines.

## Compliance calendar
Use rows **Obligation | Authority | Reporting period | Due date | Amount expected | Responsible person | Evidence lodged | Paid date**. At least monthly, reconcile it against payroll, accounts and the cash forecast.

## Strong approval routine
A payment should have an authorised purpose, evidence, budget or business justification, correct bank details independently verified where changed, and a clear record of the approver. Small teams can separate preparation from approval even when perfect segregation is impractical.

## Exercise
Prepare a one-page compliance inventory: business registration, insurance, licences, taxation, records, contracts and employment. For each mark **applies**, **not applicable with reason** or **needs confirmation**. Assign a date for every unresolved item.

*This chapter is general education. Obtain current official guidance and professional legal, tax or accounting advice.*


## Extended study: professional practice

### Separate business form from trading ambition

A sole trader and a company can both operate sophisticated enterprises; the legal structure is not a quality rating. Evaluate legal identity, ownership, liability, governance, taxation, funding and future exit plans. A sole trader is not legally separate from the individual in the same manner as a company. A company can introduce reporting and director responsibilities. Trust arrangements vary and require proper deeds and administration. A partnership introduces shared relationships and risks. Seek advice before making the choice, especially where licences, employees, assets or investors are involved.

### Design a complete records index

List core record classes: income evidence, purchase invoices, contracts, bank reconciliations, asset history, payroll, tax filings, insurance, licences, customer obligations and board or management approvals as applicable. Each has a record owner, permitted viewers, file location and retention rule. For tax, the ATO explains that most business records generally need to be kept for at least five years, with exceptions and some longer requirements. For Fair Work employee time and wage records, the stated retention period is seven years. Do not collapse these separate obligations into a single universal rule. Keep backups and test retrieval.

### Check GST with actual evidence

Whether GST registration is compulsory or voluntarily chosen depends on current rules and circumstances. Determine the business's status from authoritative records rather than the amount in its bank account. If registered, model GST collected and eligible input tax credits in the correct BAS periods under the applicable accounting basis. A customer payment that includes GST is not identical to the business's net revenue. A business expense receipt may include recoverable GST but not always. Validate invoices and classifications with a registered adviser when uncertain; mistakes can accumulate quietly across many small transactions.

### Treat payroll as a regulated process

Before a first hire, confirm the employment category, applicable award or agreement, permitted hours, minimum rates, super obligations, leave, insurance and required records. Employee cost includes more than the advertised wage. The Fair Work Ombudsman says employers generally must issue payslips within one working day and retain employee time and wage records for seven years. Payday Super began on 1 July 2026: ATO material states contributions are associated with each payday and generally must reach the employee's fund within seven business days, subject to exceptions. Confirm current guidance and your exact obligations before setting payroll schedules.

### Privacy and security of business records

Confidential information requires a role-based access model, especially customer records, employee files and supplier negotiations. Use individual accounts and multifactor authentication, a defined backup schedule, controlled sharing and a secure process to revoke access on departure. Do not store unencrypted credentials in worksheets or email them for convenience. A small business can manage access with a simple matrix listing role, record class, permitted use and approval owner. Periodically review who retains access to financial and customer systems. This is relevant to both business continuity and commercial trust.

### A compliance calendar that actually works

Calendar entries must include obligation, period, responsible person, internal preparation deadline, official lodgement date, expected cash amount, evidence location and escalation contact. Schedule preparation before the external date so discrepancies can be corrected. A workflow for each filing should check inputs, review calculations, submit through an authorised account, save acknowledgement and match payment to the banking record. When circumstances change, for example registering for GST or employing staff, trigger a compliance inventory update rather than hoping the annual accountant meeting will catch it.

### Official source register

For authoritative checking use [ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/) for tax and super; [Fair Work record keeping](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/pay-and-wages/paying-wages/record-keeping) for employment records; [ASIC](https://asic.gov.au/) for company governance; and [business.gov.au](https://business.gov.au/) for practical Australian business administration. Do not lift complex obligations from older slide decks without checking the effective date. Record the date checked and whether you relied on a primary regulator, a qualified professional, or merely an internal note.
