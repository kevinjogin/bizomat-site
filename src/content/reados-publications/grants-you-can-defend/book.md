## About this book

A grant is public money given to someone else to achieve something the government wants done. That makes grants unusually exposed. The money leaves the agency, the work is done by people the agency does not employ, and the choice of who receives it is visible to every applicant who missed out. A grant that cannot be explained later, from the record, is a grant that cannot be defended.

This book follows one grant round from start to finish. It begins with what counts as a grant and the rules that govern it, then moves through program design, grant opportunity guidelines, the choice of selection process, assessment, advice to the decision-maker, agreements and payments, monitoring and acquittal, publication and records, and finally evaluation. Each chapter ends with an exercise and a worked answer.

The chapters use **the Regional Community Facilities Program of the Northfield Regional Infrastructure Office**, a fictional agency, as a continuous example. Its grants team, applicants, grantees, amounts, scores and results are invented for teaching. They are not a real program, agency or applicant, a BIZOMAT project or a claim about how any agency works. The program's own settings, such as its delegation limits, weightings and payment schedule, are the fictional agency's choices and not Commonwealth rules.

Amounts are in Australian dollars. Grant amounts in the case exclude GST unless a figure says otherwise.

## An independent treatment

This is general education, not legal, financial or probity advice, and not an official publication of any agency. Rules change, and every agency adds its own Accountable Authority Instructions, delegations, templates and procedures. Before you act, check the version of each rule in force and your agency's own instructions, and ask your grants, finance, legal or probity area when the consequences matter.
