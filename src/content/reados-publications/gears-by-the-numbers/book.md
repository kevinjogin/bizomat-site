## About this book

Most machines buy their gears inside a gearbox, and most gearbox decisions are about catalogue ratings. Sooner or later, though, a designer has to draw a pair of gears, a machinist has to cut or check one, or a maintenance engineer has to decide whether a worn pair can be replaced with a different supplier's parts. At that point, the numbers inside the gear matter: how module and tooth count set the size, why the centre distance must match, where backlash comes from, how strong the teeth are and how to measure what was made.

This book works through those numbers in a practical order. It follows one fictional design throughout: a **single-stage spur reduction for a small conveyor drive**, with a 3 kW motor turning at 1,440 rpm and a 20-tooth pinion driving a 60-tooth gear of module 3 mm with a 20° pressure angle. The drive, its loads and its results are invented for teaching. They are not KEVOS® projects or design approvals.

Every calculation is shown in full and was checked independently. The strength checks in Chapters 8 and 9 are screening methods with explicit simplifications. They show how load, geometry and speed drive tooth stresses; they do not replace a gear rating to a recognised standard or a supplier's application review for a real drive.

## An original quantitative companion

READOS already covers selecting gearboxes and geared motors, bearings, shafts and couplings in the [Machine Elements and Mechanical Design](/reados/collections/machine-elements-and-mechanical-design/) collection. This book is the geometry and calculation companion for anyone who needs to work inside the gear rather than choose the box around it.

The explanations, the conveyor case and all worked examples are original KEVOS® writing, derived from standard involute relationships. Machinery's Handbook's gearing coverage helped identify the gap, and published references are cited for the strength and measurement methods. No tables, figures or text from any source are reproduced. Use your gear supplier's data and the applicable standards for real designs.
