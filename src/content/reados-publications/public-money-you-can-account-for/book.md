## About this book

A business manager in an Australian Government agency keeps the money, the buying, the contracts, the risks and the records of a work area in order, so the people doing the program's work can get on with it. The job is mostly judgement inside rules. The rules come from Parliament, the Department of Finance and the agency's own accountable authority. The judgement is about what to approve, what to escalate, what to report and how to say it.

This book follows that work through ten chapters. It starts with where the business manager sits in the Commonwealth framework, then moves through budgets, approvals, procurement, contracts, risk, integrity and records, the team, plans and reports, and finally advice, stakeholders and the first ninety days in the role. Each chapter ends with an exercise and a worked answer.

The chapters use **the Asset Programs Branch of the Northfield Regional Infrastructure Office**, a fictional agency, as a continuous example. Its business manager, budget, staff, suppliers, contracts and results are invented for teaching. They are not a real agency, a BIZOMAT project or a claim about how any agency works. Delegation limits in the case are the fictional agency's own and are not Commonwealth rules.

Amounts are in Australian dollars. The Commonwealth Procurement Rules state their thresholds and values inclusive of GST, and the case follows that convention unless a figure says otherwise.

## An independent treatment

This is general education, not legal, financial or employment advice, and not an official publication of any agency. Law and policy change, and every agency adds its own Accountable Authority Instructions, delegations and procedures. Before you act, check the version of each rule in force and your agency's own instructions, and ask your finance, procurement, legal or HR area when the consequences matter.
