A recruitment decision outlasts most decisions a team leader makes. The person chosen may stay for years, and the way they were chosen tells the rest of the team how decisions are made. In the Australian Public Service it is also a legal decision. It must be based on merit, it is made by someone who holds the power to make it, and some candidates can have it reviewed.

This is an illustration. Daniel Kim, one of the section's three APS 5 Client Services Officers, left in late October 2026 on promotion to another agency. His position sat in Marcus Tan's phones and email team: escalated calls, the harder emails and day-to-day help for the APS 4 officers. The workload plan in Chapter 2 showed that the team could not carry the gap for long. This chapter follows Hannah Lee through her first merit selection, from the decision to fill the position to the new officer's first day.

## What merit means in the Act

Section 10A of the [Public Service Act 1999](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00538/latest/text) sets out the APS Employment Principles. One is that the APS "makes decisions relating to engagement and promotion that are based on merit" (s 10A(1)(c)). Another is that it provides workplaces "free from discrimination, patronage and favouritism" (s 10A(1)(f)).

Section 10A(2) says when a decision is based on merit. It has five elements, and each one becomes a task for the panel:

- all eligible members of the community had a reasonable opportunity to apply;
- the candidates' relative suitability was assessed through a competitive selection process;
- the assessment compared their work-related qualities with those "genuinely required" for the duties;
- it focused on their relative capacity to achieve outcomes related to the duties;
- the assessment was the primary consideration in the decision.

The Commissioner's Directions 2022 define work-related qualities broadly (s 5), from skills and qualifications to personal qualities such as integrity and the ability to contribute to team performance. "Contributes to the team" is a quality a panel can assess. "Someone we already know" is not.

## Settle the job before you advertise it

Section 24(1) of the [Commissioner's Directions 2022](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2022L00088/latest/text) says a selection process meets the merit requirements if all four of these apply:

- "the aim and purpose of the selection process is determined in advance";
- "information about the selection process is readily available to candidates";
- the process "is applied fairly in relation to each eligible candidate";
- "the selection process is appropriately documented".

Section 24(2) adds that merit "is the primary consideration". Secondary considerations may be weighed only between candidates otherwise equal on merit, and only if they are within the candidate's control, such as being able to start by a particular date.

This is an illustration. Before anything is advertised, Hannah writes a one-page aim and purpose, dated Wednesday 4 November 2026. It describes the duties from the workload plan, not from an old position description, and sets four criteria genuinely required for them:

1. resolving complex enquiries and complaints by phone and email;
2. clear, accurate writing;
3. accurate records and confident use of client systems;
4. supporting and guiding other officers.

It names the methods: a two-page application, a 40-minute work sample (a reply to a difficult complaint email), a structured interview of five questions, and referee checks for every candidate in the range for an offer. It also names the panel and the delegate. Under NRIO's fictional delegations, the Director, Corporate and Client Services, engages and promotes staff at APS 1 to 6. The panel recommends; the Director decides.

Knowledge of NRIO's own records system is not on the list. New officers learn it in their first weeks, so it is not genuinely required, and asking for it would favour internal applicants.

## Advertise so that everyone eligible can apply

Section 25 of the Directions turns "a reasonable opportunity to apply" into rules. The vacancy must be notified in the Public Service Gazette within the 18 months before the "written decision" to engage or promote (s 25(1)). The notice must be open to all eligible members of the community, with a closing date "at least 7 calendar days after the notification", unless the agency head approves a shorter period for special circumstances (s 25(2)). An external advertisement must appear within four weeks either side of the notice, with the same closing date (s 25(7)). The Gazette can be reached through APSJobs.

For cost or operational efficiency, a non-SES vacancy may instead be opened only to eligible employees, broadly people already in the APS (s 25(4), (9)). That narrows the field, so it is never a default.

This is an illustration. People Branch runs the administration. The Gazette notice and the APSJobs advertisement go out on Thursday 12 November 2026, open to all eligible members of the community, and close on Thursday 26 November, 14 days later: seven days is a floor, not a target. The advertisement gives the duties, criteria, methods, timing and a contact officer, which is what "readily available" information looks like.

The notice also says the RecruitAbility Scheme applies. Under section 32 of the Directions, a candidate with disability who opts in is short-listed for the next stage if they have the work-related qualities required for the duties and meet any eligibility requirements. The job need not go to someone who opted in (s 32(2)). Thirty-one people apply.

## A panel that is impartial and can show it

This is an illustration. Hannah chairs the panel. Marcus joins as the team leader who knows the work. Owen Fraser, an EL1 from another branch of NRIO, is the independent member: he does not work in Client Services and will not supervise the successful candidate. People Branch advises on process but does not score.

Section 13(7) of the Act requires every APS employee to "take reasonable steps to avoid any conflict of interest (real or apparent)" and to disclose any material personal interest. The Act prescribes no form. NRIO's fictional procedure asks each panel member to declare in writing, once the applicant list is known and before short-listing, any relationship with an applicant. The delegate decides how each one is managed.

Marcus declares that he supervised one applicant, an NRIO APS 4 from another branch, for six months in 2024 during a temporary assignment to Client Services. They have no personal or financial ties. The Director records that Marcus may stay, because it was an ordinary working relationship that ended in 2024, and that Owen will call that candidate's referees. A declaration that changes nothing still shows the panel looked.

## Assess the duties, the same way for everyone

A competitive assessment compares candidates with one another against the criteria, rather than sorting them into suitable and unsuitable.

This is an illustration. The panel short-lists six candidates, A to F, on their applications. Candidate E opted into RecruitAbility and has the work-related qualities required, so E goes through. From Tuesday 8 to Thursday 10 December, all six do the same timed work sample and answer the same five questions. Each member scores each criterion on NRIO's fictional 1 to 5 scale before the panel compares notes. The panel then agrees a score for each criterion and writes down why.

Three habits keep an assessment fair:

- **Score the evidence, not the impression.** A 4 for writing points to the work sample and the example given, not to "seemed articulate".
- **Ask every candidate what they need to take part,** such as extra time or a different format, and record what was provided. That is good practice; ask People Branch what your agency's procedure requires.
- **Treat totals as a summary.** Two candidates can both score 15 with different strengths, so explain the comparison in words.

Referee checks follow the same rule: the same questions for every candidate in the range for an offer.

## Write the decision down

Section 24(1)(d) asks for a process that is "appropriately documented", and section 25(1) refers to the "written decision". The Directions prescribe no form. What is appropriate is what someone would need to check that the process met the rules: the delegate now, and a reviewer later. The selection report should let the delegate make the decision rather than sign the panel's.


This is an illustration. The Director reads the final report against the aim and purpose and signs the written decision on Thursday 17 December 2026. The successful candidate, Tessa Morgan, is an ongoing APS 4 in another agency. Because she is an ongoing APS employee moving up a classification group, the decision is a promotion, not an engagement, and that changes what happens next.

## Merit lists, the Gazette and promotion review

One selection process can fill more than one vacancy. The Public Service Regulations 2023 define merit pools, ranked merit lists and ranked merit pools, in which candidates of similar suitability are grouped and the groups ranked (s 5). Under section 25(1)(b) of the Directions, a later decision can rely on the original notice if the new vacancy is a "similar vacancy" and the notice fell within the 18 months before that decision. Section 9 requires the same category (ongoing or non-ongoing), similar work-related qualities, the same classification and, among other options, the same agency.

Promotions carry two further steps. The agency head must notify the promotion of an ongoing APS employee in the Gazette within three months after the decision (Directions s 40). And section 20 of the [Public Service Regulations 2023](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2023L00368/latest/text) gives a right to promotion review by the Merit Protection Commissioner, but only for vacancies in "Groups 2 to 6 set out in Schedule 1 to the Classification Rules", that is, APS Levels 2 to 6. Executive Levels 1 and 2 are Groups 7 and 8.

```figure

- Not an ongoing APS employee | No promotion review | External and non-ongoing candidates can ask for feedback instead

- The vacancy is EL1 or EL2 | No promotion review | Groups 7 and 8 are outside section 20; other review is limited to serious defects

caption: Public Service Regulations 2023, sections 20, 21 and 37. Ongoing Parliamentary Service employees are treated much like APS employees here: see section 20(2) and (3).
```

The application must reach the Merit Protection Commissioner by "5 pm of the 14th day after the day the decision is notified in the Public Service Gazette", and making it "operates to stay" the decision (s 21). If the Merit Protection Commissioner finds that the process did not meet the merit requirements, the promotion does not take effect, unless the outcome would not have been materially different. For an applicant who is an APS employee, that determination binds the agency (s 24(2)(a), (3) and (6)). This is why the record matters. The review period also sets the start date. Under section 42 of the Directions, if no application is made by the deadline, a promotion takes effect on the 14th day after it, or on a later day agreed in time.

## Short-term and non-ongoing engagement

Not every gap needs a full process. Section 22(2) of the Act allows engagement as an ongoing employee, for a specified term or task, or for irregular or intermittent duties, and section 13 of the Regulations sets when a specified term is allowed, such as cover for an employee on leave. Chapter 2 looked at when each suits the work. For a non-ongoing engagement of 12 months or less, section 27 of the Directions modifies the merit process, as the figure shows.

```figure
type: compare
title: Two routes into the team
cols: Ongoing, or non-ongoing beyond 12 months | Non-ongoing for 12 months or less
rowhead: Requirement
caption: Directions sections 24, 25 and 27, and Regulations section 13. Ask your People area which route applies before you start.
- Notice | Gazette notice, normally open to all eligible members of the community, closing at least 7 calendar days later | Brought to the community's notice as far as practicable
- Process | Aim set in advance, information available, applied fairly, appropriately documented | A modified process under section 27
- Standard | Relative suitability, with merit the primary consideration | At least the work-related qualities genuinely required
- Afterwards | A similar vacancy within 18 months of the notice | Extensions, up to 18 months in total
```

Two cautions. A person engaged short-term to cover a gap competes for the ongoing role on merit like everyone else. And a non-ongoing employee cannot simply be made ongoing: section 30 of the Directions allows that only with the Commissioner's authorisation, in exceptional circumstances, and only if the original engagement went through a full merit process.

## When it goes wrong: a panel that had already chosen

This is an illustration of a process NRIO did not run. Suppose that, in the week Daniel's move was confirmed, a former colleague rings Hannah from her old agency. An APS 4 officer there, whom Hannah worked alongside for two years and rates highly, wants a move.

Here is how that goes wrong if Hannah decides before the process starts. The criteria come from the officer's résumé, including experience with the complaints software her old agency uses. The notice runs for the minimum seven days. Hannah chairs the panel and declares nothing. The officer scores highly, the report says she "stood out", and nobody else's referees are called. The Director signs.

After the Gazette notice, an ongoing APS 4 who was ranked second learns from a mutual contact that Hannah knew the successful candidate well. She applies to the Merit Protection Commissioner before the deadline, and the promotion is stayed. The reviewer asks NRIO for the documents (Regulations s 22(5)). The aim and purpose is dated after applications closed. One criterion was not genuinely required for the duties. The scores were written after the panel's discussion. Hannah's two years working with the officer appear nowhere. If the Merit Protection Commissioner finds the process fell short, the promotion does not take effect unless the outcome would have been the same anyway.

The team carries the vacancy into autumn. The officer, who may well have been the best candidate, loses the job and her good name. Hannah's undeclared interest becomes a question under section 13(7) for People Branch. And the team has learned how jobs are filled.

Every control in this chapter would have caught it: a dated aim and purpose before the notice; criteria tested against the duties; a written declaration before short-listing, which would have put the question of Hannah's role to the Director; scores recorded before discussion; the same checks for everyone; and a delegate who reads the report against the aim. Simplest of all, Hannah could have told her old colleague that the job would be on APSJobs, open to anyone.

## Exercise: review the draft selection report

This is an illustration. On Friday 11 December 2026 the panel's first draft of the selection report reaches Hannah. She reviews it against the aim and purpose before it goes to the Director. B and C are ongoing APS 4 officers in other agencies, F is an ongoing APS 4 at NRIO, and A, D and E are not APS employees. Find everything that needs fixing, and say what the corrected report should recommend.

| Part | What the draft says |
| --- | --- |
| Aim and purpose | Attached, dated Wednesday 4 November 2026 |
| Declarations | "Nil." Marcus's declaration about Candidate F and the Director's note are on file |
| Scores, criteria 1 to 4 | A: 4, 3, 4, 3 = 14. B: 5, 4, 4, 3 = 15. C: 4, 4, 4, 3 = 15. D: 4, 4, 4, 3 = 15. E: 3, 4, 3, 3 = 13. F: 3, 3, 4, 3 = 13 |
| Recommendation | "B, C and D are equal on 15. We recommend D, who can start sooner." |
| Referee checks | Two referees for D; none for anyone else |
| Merit list | "All six are suitable. The list may be used for any APS 4 or APS 5 role in the branch, ongoing or non-ongoing, until 31 December 2028." |
| Review | "Unsuccessful candidates may seek review within 14 days of being told the outcome." |

### Worked answer

**Scores.** B's total is wrong: 5 + 4 + 4 + 3 = 16, not 15. The rest are right: A is 4 + 3 + 4 + 3 = 14; C and D are each 4 + 4 + 4 + 3 = 15; E is 3 + 4 + 3 + 3 = 13; F is 3 + 3 + 4 + 3 = 13. There is no three-way tie, and each candidate's scores should be explained in words.

**Recommendation.** Merit is the primary consideration (Directions s 24(2)(a)). A start date is a secondary consideration, usable only between candidates otherwise equal on merit (s 24(2)(b)). B is ahead, so the panel recommends B. D can start sooner partly because engaging someone from outside the APS involves no Gazette notice of the decision and no review period. That is no reason to prefer D.

**Referee checks.** B is recommended, and C and D are next, so all three need checks with the same questions. Checking only one is not applying the process fairly to each candidate (s 24(1)(c)). If a check changes the picture, the panel revisits the ranking and records why.

**Declarations.** "Nil" is wrong. The report should record Marcus's declaration, when he made it, the Director's decision that he could stay, and Owen's referee calls for F.

**Merit list.** The ranking can fill only a similar vacancy (Directions s 9): ongoing, APS 5, similar work-related qualities, at NRIO. It covers written decisions within 18 months of the notice of Thursday 12 November 2026, so until May 2028; People Branch confirms the last day. The order is B; then C and D, equal; then A; then E and F, equal. With two pairs grouped as equally suitable, the result is a ranked merit pool (Regulations s 5).

**Review.** Only applicants for whom the job would be a promotion, ongoing APS employees at APS 4 or below, can seek promotion review (Regulations s 20(1)): C and F, and any such applicant among the 25 not short-listed. A, D and E cannot. The right exists because the decision promotes B, an APS employee. The deadline runs from the Gazette notice, not from when candidates are told. For a notice on Thursday 7 January 2027, the 14th day after is Thursday 21 January, so applications close at 5 pm that day.

Only the aim and purpose could go to the Director as drafted. The corrected report recommends B, Tessa Morgan, with C and D next: the report the Director signed on 17 December.
