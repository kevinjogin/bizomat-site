## About this book

Refrigerators, air conditioners, heat pumps and air compressors all do one thing that heat will not do by itself: they move heat, or energy, in a direction it would not flow naturally, and they pay for it in electricity. How much they must pay is limited by the second law of thermodynamics, and the limit depends almost entirely on temperatures. That makes these machines unusually predictable by numbers: a few temperatures tell you the best a machine could possibly do, and a realistic fraction of that best tells you what to expect. The same numbers explain why a dirty condenser costs money, why a heat pump that is efficient for warm water is poor for very hot water, and why a cold room's door discipline matters.

This book works these machines through by numbers. It starts with the second law and the Carnot limits, defines the coefficient of performance, walks through the vapour-compression cycle, evaluates a heat pump for a factory's hot water, builds up a cold room's refrigeration load, explains what drives running costs, applies the same thinking to air compressors and their waste heat, and finishes with the rules for refrigerants. Each chapter works one question through the running case, shows a failure and how it was found, and ends with an exercise and its worked answer.

## The running case

The book follows two pieces of plant at a fictional **food factory**, the same factory whose hot water and steam systems appear in *Heat Transfer by the Numbers* and *Process Steam by the Numbers*. The first is a proposal to supply part of the wash-down hot water system's heat, about **150,000 kWh** a year at up to 85 °C, from a heat pump instead of gas. The second is **CS-1**, a chilled store held at **2 °C**, 6 m by 5 m by 3 m, receiving about **2,000 kg** of product a day.

All quantities, prices and results are invented for teaching. This is an illustration. None of it describes a KEVOS® client, supplier or project. Heat from gas is costed at about **A$0.093** per kWh delivered, as in the companion books, and electricity at an assumed **A$0.25** per kWh. Money is in Australian dollars, excluding GST. Real machines' performance comes from their makers' data at the actual operating conditions; this book's ratio of real to ideal performance, 0.5, is a teaching value.

## How to use the book

Chapters 1 to 3 build the ideas: the second law, COP and the cycle. Chapters 4 and 5 apply them to the two pieces of plant. Chapter 6 covers running costs and maintenance, Chapter 7 compressed air and heat recovery, and Chapter 8 refrigerants and safety.

## Source limits and professional responsibility

The supplied thermodynamics notes informed the range of subjects. The methods here are standard thermodynamics, simplified for hand calculation, with sources for material data. Refrigerating systems and heat pumps are regulated: handling fluorocarbon refrigerants requires a licence, and system design and installation follow the applicable standards and work health and safety law. Some refrigerants are flammable or toxic. Real systems must be designed, installed and serviced by appropriately licensed and competent people. This book is educational and is not a design manual.
